# Exhibitions: historical sources and collection plan

Research updated: **12 October 2026**. Current scope: **historical exhibitions from 1600 through 1989**, with paintings, drawings, prints and icons. The user replaced the volume-first, all-years collection direction. Photography, sculpture and unrelated object categories are excluded from new exhibition collection. Existing catalogue cleanup and source coverage are tracked in `research/historical-exhibitions-20261012/README.md`.

This is the source register and research plan for the new **Exhibitions** category. It contains 57 source families. The initial research pass imported no records. The subsequently authorized production collection is tracked in the [100,000-exhibition campaign](research/exhibitions-100k-20261011/README.md); provider totals below are not import receipts or claims of complete museum coverage.

## Scope and counting

- Research every museum in Artline, across its full documented history, including predecessor names, former buildings, branches and exhibitions organized elsewhere.
- Include exhibitions without a museum: commercial galleries, artist societies, Salons, academies, independent spaces, touring shows, biennials and documented online exhibitions. A missing museum link must not prevent an exhibition record.
- Exhibition dates must fall within **1600–1989**. An exhibition held in 2026 is outside this collection even when it displayed Renaissance paintings. New main-catalogue artworks retain their separate creation-date and selection rules. Unknown or boundary-crossing exhibition dates require source review; do not invent a date.
- Count a recurring exhibition's individual editions separately. Count a documented touring exhibition once, with separate venue occurrences. A substantially different exhibition sharing a title needs separate identity review.
- Preserve separately titled, independently documented exhibitions within larger festivals using a parent relationship. Do not count pavilions, sections, catalogue volumes or venue stops as additional exhibitions merely to increase the total.
- A publication, digitized copy, review, artist appearance and checklist entry are different entities. None automatically contributes one exhibition to the target.
- Record announced, cancelled and uncertain exhibitions, but exclude them from the **confirmed-held** target until supported. Record physical, online and hybrid formats separately.
- Historical exhibition participation does not establish ownership or current display. Keep organizers, hosts, lenders, present-day owners and critics distinct.

## Where to start

| Priority | Sources | Why they come first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | MoMA CSV, Art Institute API, DoME, Royal Academy, INHA Salons, RKD excerpts | Stable identifiers or catalogue numbers; strong opportunities to establish events and recover artwork lists. |
| 2 | Art Platform Japan, BasArt/ARTL@s, museum exhibition archives | Broader institutional and geographic coverage. Verify export routes and coverage before estimating import volume. |
| 3 | Getty Research Portal, Internet Archive, Gallica, Heidelberg, HathiTrust, INHA library | Historical exhibition catalogues, scans and OCR; often the best route to individual exhibited works. |
| 4 | Critics' archives, art bibliographies, dealer papers and artist archives | Recover missing exhibitions, reviews, alternate titles, dates, lenders and object identities. |
| 5 | Artfacts and artist-info | Large discovery networks; bulk access is not established here, and artist participation is not an artwork checklist. |

Evidence labels below: **E** = exhibition identity/dates/venue; **A** = individual artwork entries or histories may be available; **P** = artist/curator participation; **R** = reviews/critical reception; **D** = bibliographic or archival discovery. An A label describes source capability, not complete artwork coverage for every exhibition.

Access descriptions distinguish publisher-documented capabilities from tested retrieval. Public search access does not imply a working bulk export. Unless stated otherwise, bulk retrieval has not been tested in this pass.

## How to recover which artworks were exhibited

1. **Establish the event.** Capture title as printed, translated/alternative titles, organizer, actual venue, date wording and source identifiers. Separate exhibition dates from catalogue publication dates and review dates. Keep date precision; a year-only source does not justify inventing 1 January.
2. **Find the checklist.** Search the official exhibition page, catalogue, annual report, final checklist, installation documentation and artwork exhibition histories. Library records locate publications but rarely establish their complete contents.
3. **Extract entries with page evidence.** Save catalogue number, creator label, title, medium, dimensions, artwork date wording, lender/owner wording and accession or catalogue-raisonné identifiers where printed. Retain scan sequence, printed page and source version alongside OCR.
4. **Record what the evidence says.** Distinguish `exhibited`, `catalogued`, `illustrated_only`, `discussed_only`, `proposed`, `withdrawn` and `uncertain`. A published checklist supports catalogued participation; an explicit exhibition history or final installation/loan record can support actual exhibition. Retain conflicting claims instead of overwriting them.
5. **Resolve the same object.** Prefer accession numbers, catalogue-raisonné references and supported lender/provenance chains; compare dimensions, medium, title variants and versions. Artist plus a common title is insufficient. A print impression, copy, study or detail must not silently become the better-known original.
6. **Keep unresolved entries.** An exhibition entry may exist without an Artline artwork or painter ID. Preserve anonymous creators, workshops and disputed attributions as source labels. An exhibition can remain useful even with no resolved artwork links.
7. **Check the particular venue.** Travelling exhibitions can have different checklists at different stops. Attach a work to an occurrence only when supported, otherwise retain tour-level evidence without implying it appeared everywhere.
8. **Measure completeness.** Mark the checklist complete, partial, unavailable or unknown, with the basis for that judgment. A catalogue with 200 entries and only 12 matched artworks still has 188 unresolved entries, not a 12-work exhibition.

For OCR, review title pages, date ranges, headings and entry boundaries before automated matching. Common errors include merged columns, catalogue numbers read as years, historical spellings and owner names mistaken for artists. Store the original transcription and corrections separately.

Illustrations in catalogues, installation photographs and artworks already in Artline can support research. Reproduction selection remains under the existing [image-use workflow](ARTLINE_IMAGE_USE.md); collecting a catalogue reference does not require downloading every image in the book.

## Proposed records for the Exhibitions category

This is a proposed structure for a later migration, not an existing schema.

| Record | Minimum purpose and relationships |
| --- | --- |
| `exhibitions` | Distinct exhibition/edition identity; original and alternative titles; series/parent where supported; physical/online/hybrid format; held/announced/cancelled/uncertain state; evidence. No required museum ID. |
| `exhibition_occurrences` | One venue stop, with nullable `institution_id`, original venue label, location, historical address if known, date text/precision and supported date bounds. Organizer and host are separate roles. |
| `exhibition_entries` | Source checklist number and original object/creator/lender labels; nullable `artwork_id`; optional occurrence scope; participation claims and their evidence. Keep unresolved entries. |
| `exhibition_participants` | People/organizations and explicit roles such as artist, curator, organizer, lender or catalogue editor. Artist participation does not imply every artwork by that artist was shown. |
| `publications` and `exhibition_publications` | Catalogue, checklist, review or other source; author/editor, publication date, edition, identifiers, library copies and digitization links. One book can cover multiple exhibitions; one exhibition can have multiple books. |
| `exhibition_reviews` | Critic, original byline, review publication, pages/date, referenced exhibition and supported artwork references. Separate the reviewer's claims from catalogue facts. |
| `exhibition_evidence` | Claim/relationship supported, source ID/URL, record ID, page/catalogue number, original wording, retrieval date, evidence type, uncertainty and snapshot/hash when captured. |
| `exhibition_source_coverage` | Museum/source/year-range research ledger, access state, extraction progress and remaining gaps. |

Use source namespaces and source record IDs for repeatable imports. Cross-source reconciliation needs title aliases, venue identity, dates and edition information; a normalized title alone is not a safe deduplication key. Keep merge decisions and original IDs in audit history.

Artline's existing [Events design](events-timeline.md) is for world history through 2000, with contextual artwork links. Its [`0022_events_catalogue.sql`](../apps/server/db/migrations/0022_events_catalogue.sql) constrains that date range. Exhibitions need their own relationship model and all-years handling; do not force new exhibitions into that historical Events contract. Historical museum-holding context likewise does not supply an exhibition identity.

Apply the unified catalogue policy to future active exhibition records: retain review state and uncertainty as audit data without recreating the removed research/published browsing split. Preserve existing artwork metadata, holdings and images when adding exhibition evidence. Counts, filters, joins and pagination belong in Go/PostgreSQL, with bounded responses to the browser.

## Collection sequence toward 100,000

1. **Inventory museum coverage.** Read existing institution IDs and aliases without modifying the real local catalogue. Create one research ledger row per museum, including those for which no historical exhibition source has yet been located. Add independent venues as supported labels without forcing museum records.
2. **Prove a mixed pilot of 500–1,000 distinct exhibitions.** Use the MoMA CSV, selected catalogue projects and accessible museum resources. Include a tour, a recurring edition, a museum-free exhibition, a historical attribution, an unknown date and an incomplete checklist. This is a proposed pilot size, not a delivered count.
3. **Expand structured sources.** Retrieve supported exports/APIs with resumable checkpoints and source snapshots. Resolve the BasArt access failure and Art Institute timeout before treating those paths as operational. Measure unique events, completeness and overlap for every batch.
4. **Extend each museum backwards through time.** Combine web archives with annual reports, catalogues, institutional finding aids and object exhibition histories. Track gaps by decade; a museum's first online listing is not its first exhibition.
5. **Add galleries, societies and independent exhibitions.** Use library catalogue runs, dealer papers, artists' archives, biennials and critics' references. Follow archival evidence to the catalogue or artwork record wherever possible.
6. **Recover artwork lists at scale.** Process selected catalogues with OCR and page-level evidence, preserving unresolved entries. Batch candidate artwork matching by creator, institution or IDs; review ambiguity before attaching a link. Audit both successful links and unmatched/withdrawn entries.
7. **Reconcile and verify before counting completion.** Exclude duplicate sources, translated aliases, repeated venue stops, cancelled/planned events and non-exhibition records. Preserve those records and decisions where useful; do not delete source evidence to simplify a total.

Use a progress report with separate measures:

- **Distinct confirmed-held exhibitions** — the 100,000 target.
- Venue occurrences, independent-venue exhibitions, physical/online formats and geography/date coverage.
- Museums inventoried, museums researched, museums with documented exhibitions and remaining historical gaps.
- Exhibitions with complete, partial or unavailable checklists.
- Documentary artwork entries, resolved Artline artwork links and unresolved entries.
- Reviews linked to exhibitions, identified critics and catalogues located/digitized.
- Duplicates reconciled, conflicting claims, proposed/withdrawn works and source-access failures.

Keep raw fetched records, normalized records and accepted relationships separately, with repeatable checkpoints. Prefer provider-supported exports/APIs and bounded retrieval; a failed or unavailable endpoint remains a recorded gap, not an invitation to count its advertised inventory as collected. This source register does not clear existing host holds or establish subscription access.
