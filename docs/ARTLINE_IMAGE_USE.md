# Artline image use

On 17 September 2026 the user confirmed Artline is a **public or potentially commercial app**. Image review must support that use; private study or noncommercial permission alone is insufficient.

For each selected reproduction, preserve evidence for the underlying artwork, the exact photograph and its origin, and any separate custodian or cultural-property permission that applies. A public-domain artwork or a Creative Commons photograph does not by itself establish every required permission. Conversely, a museum's contract for supplying its own photographs must not automatically be extended to independently licensed visitor photographs.

CC0, public-domain reproductions, CC BY and CC BY-SA can support commercial use when the evidence and other applicable permissions support it. Preserve required credits, source and licence links, change notices and ShareAlike conditions. Do not add restrictions that conflict with the image licence. Noncommercial-only, personal-study-only and unresolved permission cases remain held.

For Italian public cultural property, assess commercial reproduction clearance separately from photographic copyright. The Ministry's [national guidance, section 3.2.3](https://docs.italia.it/italia/icdp/icdp-pnd-circolazione-riuso-docs/it/v1.0-giugno-2022/acquisizione-circolazione-e-riuso-delle-riproduzioni-dei-beni-culturali-in-ambiente-digitale/tipologie-duso-delle-riproduzioni-di-beni-culturali.html) addresses paid commercial applications. Its [2024 fee guidelines](https://asmo.cultura.gov.it/fileadmin/risorse/normativa/DM_21_marzo_2024_rep._108-signed.pdf) concern institutions of the Ministry; do not assume their tariffs apply to municipal collections, other public institutions or private museums. A public app is not necessarily a paid app. Artline's confirmed possibility of commercial use requires review covering that possibility, rather than assuming a nonprofit exception.

The [Italy campaign follow-up](research/italy-images-4h-20260916/commercial-use-review-20260917/README.md) records the object-specific decisions. A hold concerns the unresolved permitted use and does not revoke a photographer's licence or erase its evidence. Preserve artwork records, unknown fields, provenance and review status. Image clearance does not authorize catalogue publication. No museum outreach or payment is implied by the user's use clarification.

## Explicit WikiArt collection instruction — 19–20 September 2026

For the [selected WikiArt image campaign](research/wikiart-selected-images-20260919/README.md), the user explicitly requested downloading and adding images of artworks created more than 70 years ago, then instructed that independent rights-evidence review should not block this collection task. This campaign uses a conservative year-only creation cutoff of 1955, rather than an artist death cutoff. It records WikiArt's actual per-artwork rights labels without converting artwork age into a public-domain claim or claiming an independently obtained licence. Copyright-labelled images are retained as `restricted`. On 20 September the user also explicitly instructed that attached images must remain visible publicly, including these images, with links to their sources. Image rendering therefore does not depend on rights status, verification timestamp or missing image alt text; rights labels and source links remain visible. Artwork metadata, publication state and prior image associations are preserved. This records the user's collection/display preference, not a copyright-holder permission.

The same explicit instruction applies to the [artist-first WikiArt expansion](research/wikiart-artist-coverage-20260920/README.md). Selected new artworks remain review records and enter a personal owner collection, separate from museum designations and holdings. Incomplete creator information may remain an object-level label. Application image derivatives must be at most **100,000 bytes**, resized proportionally without cropping; larger source originals remain separately archived. The existing catalogue's oversized images were also compressed while preserving their rights evidence and references.
