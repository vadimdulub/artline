Can Google index everything?

Google can discover public pages and images. It does not automatically read everything in your database, and it does not guarantee that every eligible page will be indexed.

Give important content its own accessible URL, useful text and ordinary links from related pages. A sitemap helps Google discover those URLs; indexing and prominent rankings are separate outcomes.

Yes, write articles.

Start with one carefully sourced guide each week, published on Artline under a section such as /guides/.

Your Greek, Byzantine, Russian-icon and Cypriot research gives you material for a focused editorial programme. These ideas are starting hypotheses, not measured keyword demand.

Byzantine and post-Byzantine art: an illustrated introduction

Explain the terms with selected examples, a simple chronology and links to approved artwork records.

Greek painters: a timeline through selected artworks

Connect creators and works across periods. Let readers continue exploring those connections in Artline.

Understanding Russian icons: symbols and examples

Explain a few well-supported visual details, with sources and explicit attribution where a creator is unknown.

Exploring art collections in Cyprus

Introduce documented collection highlights. Distinguish holdings from current display; verify visiting information before publication.

A useful article structure

  1. Answer one question. Use a clear title and give the reader an answer near the beginning.
  2. Show and explain. Choose a few relevant images and explain what to notice in each one.
  3. Add something original. Make a comparison, connect a timeline or explain your documented research.
  4. Make the evidence visible. Include sources, actual image credits, an honest byline and meaningful update dates.
  5. Offer a next step. Link to related approved artworks, artists and another relevant guide.

Google recommends useful, original content with clear sourcing. There is no preferred word count: people-first content guidance.

Make every visit worthwhile.

An artwork page should help a reader understand the work and decide what to explore next.

Give the work context

Show its title, creator or qualified attribution, known date, documented museum connection and sources. Keep unknown facts explicit.

Help images be understood

Use descriptive alternative text, informative captions and nearby relevant text. Preserve the image’s actual source, credit and rights evidence.

Connect related material

Offer relevant links to the artist, other works and an explanatory guide. Readers should have an obvious next step.

Make exploration comfortable

Check mobile readability, image loading, keyboard navigation and page responsiveness. Keep the main explanation easy to reach.

Give the guide a life beyond search.

Choose one social channel you can maintain and a few relevant communities where your work is useful.

  • Share a small, complete story. Use an approved image and one interesting observation, then link to the full guide.
  • Create something educators can use. A comparison of two works or a short timeline exercise gives people a practical reason to reference Artline.
  • Invite readers back. Consider an optional weekly email with one artwork and one story. Make subscribing and unsubscribing straightforward.
  • Participate where the topic belongs. Share relevant resources in art and museum communities, following their rules.

A manageable weekly rhythm

Research one question. Publish one illustrated guide. Share one useful excerpt. Review what people read and explore next.

Measure discovery and curiosity.

Use Search Console for search performance. Use suitable site analytics, if introduced, for what visitors do after arriving.

Indexed eligible pages
Are approved pages appearing in Google’s index? Investigate exclusions and errors.
Search impressions and clicks
Which questions and pages bring readers? Separate searches for “Artline” from other queries.
Image-search traffic
Check the Image search type in Search Console to see which illustrated pages get attention.
Exploration and return visits
Are readers opening another artwork, returning later or choosing to subscribe?

Review weekly, then make broader decisions over several weeks and months. A new site may have little data at first. This plan is a working strategy, not a traffic or ranking forecast.
