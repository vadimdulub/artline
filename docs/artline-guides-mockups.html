Draft

A room, a shore,
a garden

Three paintings from the 1890s give us three different ways to look at light.

Begin with what you can see

In Anna Ancher’s blue room, sunlight forms a bright patch against the wall. In Krøyer’s beach scene, pale dresses stand against the blue shore. In Monet’s garden, look between the bridge, the plants and their reflections.

Compare the three examples below. Where does your eye go first? Which edges look clear, and which seem to dissolve?

Return to one detail

Choose one painting and follow the light through it. Start with its brightest area, then look at the neighboring colors. Describe the difference before interpreting it.

Editorial note: these observations are draft interpretation. Object dates and image credits come from the existing catalogue evidence. Review the final text before publication.

Begin with what you can see

Where does your eye go first? Compare the paintings, then choose one artist to give a single work your full attention.

Return to one detail

Start with the brightest area. Follow its edge into the colors beside it. Does the boundary stay sharp, or does it soften? Take a moment to describe what you see before deciding what it means.
