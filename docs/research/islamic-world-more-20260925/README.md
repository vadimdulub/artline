## Review decisions

- The selection reuses the previous same-day, bounded 100-record Cleveland metadata response and adds two specific Met API records. Only the 20 pinned choices were downloaded. Exact accession and canonical source identities were checked against the real local catalogue using read-only queries; no matches were found.
- Eighteen new objects are held by Cleveland Museum of Art and two by the Met. Museum ownership evidence is recorded as holdings only; no on-view status is inferred.
- Each source explicitly identifies the exact reproduction as CC0/public domain. Preserved evidence includes the museum object response, image URL, metadata and image hashes, retrieval receipts, open-access policies, credits and licence links. The policy captures from the first set retain their original retrieval dates.
- All dates are museum-supplied artwork intervals, including fourteenth-century works within the preset’s wider context. Earlier book authors’ lifespans and depicted historical rulers are not substituted for illustration dates. No dates are invented.
- The Met tile panel keeps “Probably from Iran, Nishapur.” Its country filter reflects the museum attribution, with that qualification preserved in `creation_place_display` and the geography note.
- The pharmacy jar is recorded by Cleveland as from Samarkand. Mapping Samarkand to present-day Uzbekistan is corroborated by [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/603/). This geographic mapping does not assert modern nationality for a tenth-century maker. Museum origins already support the other country links.
- The Afghan salver preserves **Ibrahim the Decorator (or the Engraver)** from the museum’s reading of its inscription. The carved tombstone preserves **Abaidallah Murra(?) and Umar(?)** with its question marks and carver role. The Jajarmi manuscript preserves his documented **author and scribe** role while explicitly leaving the illustrator unrecorded. No speculative artist biographies or profiles are created.
- Other missing makers remain unrecorded. Inscriptions and prose were checked alongside the API creator arrays so that an empty array did not erase documented names.
- Each parent manuscript accession is imported once with the museum’s supplied composite or primary image; child recto/verso records are not separately imported. Existing repairs, wear and missing areas are kept in the images.
