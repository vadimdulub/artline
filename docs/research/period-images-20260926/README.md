## Selection decisions

- Early cities: six Sumerian sculptures and ritual objects from Iraq, including
  Gudea, animal figures and a carved cup. [Cleveland example](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1963.154).
- Classical world: six Greek, Roman and Etruscan objects. Greek/Etruscan labels
  without an unambiguous modern origin remain unassigned; exact artwork focus
  links include them without inventing manufacture countries.
- Buddhism: Gandharan, Mathuran, Afghan, Thai and Asuka Japanese sculpture;
  source qualifications such as “probably Tape Shotor” remain intact.
- Byzantium: Constantinopolitan ivory and metalwork plus three Egyptian
  Christian textiles. Explicit artwork links include Byzantine Egypt without
  treating the museum's present location as the object's origin.
- Islamic learning and Mongol connections: Seljuq luster and mina’i ceramics,
  Ilkhanid ceramics, carved decoration and textile, and a Yuan-dynasty jar.
- Tang–Song: nine selected mirrors, sculptures and paintings, including Li Song,
  Juran and Mi Youren. Source scrolls remain complete, without cropping.
- Sahel: a qualified Inland Niger Delta figure; both the broad date and probable
  Djenné-Djenno attribution are retained. [Museum record](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1985.199).
- Silk Roads: Iranian and Jin-dynasty textiles alongside eligible regional
  additions. Existing route-country filters, including Uzbekistan, remain.
  Ambiguously Sogdian/Chinese and Nishapur/Samarkand objects were researched but
  not assigned an invented definite origin or downloaded.
- Mughal world: seven courtly, literary and historical paintings. Multiple named
  makers remain source-qualified object labels. Bishandas is an attributed
  creator omitted from the museum's public caption; that attribution stays in
  the preserved evidence, with no accepted artist link or invented biography.
- Renaissance/Reformation: three Cranach works; the museum's own highlight
  designation makes its hunting scene eligible under Renaissance Highlights.
- Atlantic encounters: a Chimú-Inka vessel and a Mexican pendant, editorially
  selected as local visual culture spanning the time of contact. Source type
  “Jewelry” stays in evidence; the internal work type remains unknown.
- Edo: two Hokusai works; the broad source interval on the painting is retained.
- Science: Merian's plant-and-insect study. The museum's display date and numeric
  interval differ; both are preserved, without silently correcting either.
- Enlightenment/industrial: mezzotints by Pether and Earlom after Joseph Wright.
  Pether is added to the industrial creator choices with the museum source;
  engraving and “after” roles are not collapsed into primary authorship by Wright.
- French Revolution: David's 1813 study is explicitly Napoleonic-era context,
  not a claimed depiction of the Revolution.
- Romanticism/empire: a Delacroix sheet, a qualified attribution to Delacroix,
  and Gérôme's Orientalist sculpture. The attribution is not promoted to an
  accepted artist identity; the latter works enter the broader empire view.
- Modernism: Cassatt's double-sided drawing, preserved as one accession.
- First World War/interwar: Kollwitz's study of hardship in the aftermath;
  two sides remain one object, with the original approximate interval.
- Russian Revolution: Malevich's **1913** lithograph is earlier avant-garde
  context. [NGA identity and public-domain media](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/93945-peasant-woman-goes-water).
- Second World War: Nash's **1934** photograph supplies context from a selected
  war artist; it is not described or re-dated as a wartime picture.
- Civil rights: Pippin's African American domestic and spiritual subjects,
  explicitly earlier context rather than participation in the 1954–1968 movement.

## Rights, data integrity and implementation

The [Cleveland open-access policy](https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access),
[NGA open-access policy](https://www.nga.gov/terms-and-notices), and
[Barnes policy](https://www.barnesfoundation.org/collection/open-access-and-copyright)
were checked against each selected object. Each image has source receipts,
rights evidence and a checksum. Prepared files are full-frame JPEGs under
100,000 bytes; the largest in this pass is 99,736 bytes.

The primary batch adds 14 explicitly museum-designated highlight memberships
and six separate **owner/editorial** memberships. Museum holdings are documented
as holdings; **zero current-display assertions** were added. No artist profiles
were invented. Attributed makers remain qualified and unlinked unless an
unqualified primary identity was reconciled against an existing artist.

Migration `0029_textile_photograph_types.sql` permits accurate textile and
photograph classifications; it changes neither publication status nor general
import eligibility. Existing strict bulk-ingestion adapters were not relaxed.
The first import transaction rolled back completely on the old work-type check.
A retry collided with the backup's read lock and also rolled back. The final
atomic transaction succeeded after the validated backup completed. Recovery
artifacts retain the first attempt's preimages.

Pinned plans, original source captures, before/after API responses, image and
transaction receipts, exact database identities and verification results stay
in these research directories. Reproductions are local assets under
`apps/web/public/assets/artworks/imported/period-images-20260926/` and
`.../pippin-images-20260926/`. Originals and validated PostgreSQL recovery dumps
are outside Documents, under the corresponding campaign directories in:
