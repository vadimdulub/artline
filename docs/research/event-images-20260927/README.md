# Additional historical-period images — 27 September 2026

42 new, individually selected museum objects and full-frame reproductions were
added to the local review catalogue and transferred to production. The completed
Decolonization research batch is delivered separately in the same release.

| Historical preset | New artworks | Selection |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Writing and the first cities | 7 | Mesopotamian sculpture and seals; Egyptian relief, royal sculpture, funerary painting and a paint box |
| Classical world | 6 | Attic vase painting and Greek art from southern Italy |
| Buddhism | 6 | Gandhara, Sarnath, Northern Wei/Sui China, Thailand and Cambodia |
| Mughal world | 8 | Court portraits, narrative, music, animals and imaginative botanical painting |
| Tang and Song China | 8 | Landscape scrolls, sericulture, calligraphy, ceramics and mirror decoration |
| Islamic worlds and learning | 5 | Cordoba architectural carving, Nishapur pottery, Iraqi inscription and Syrian glass |
| West African trade and learning | 1 | Dogon or Bozo staff with seated male leader, Mali, 16th–17th century |
| Silk Roads | 1 | Arabic-inscribed bowl from present-day Uzbekistan, probably Samarqand, late 10th–11th century |

The [Cleveland Museum of Art open-access policy](https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access)
and each selected object's explicit CC0 flag support 40 reproductions. The two
Met objects have exact public-domain API designations under the museum's
[Open Access policy](https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/image-resources):
[staff](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/310001) and
[Uzbekistan bowl](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/449711).

Metadata searches were bounded. Thirteen already represented accessions or
identities were excluded before downloading. Met object 310948 was excluded
because its API does not provide a public-domain image. No exhaustive download,
new artist biography, publication transition, or current-display claim occurred.
Museum holdings have explicit object citations and remain distinct from display.

All source date intervals, cultural wording, attribution qualifiers and named
unreconciled makers remain in the evidence and object metadata. Modern countries
refer to recorded object origins; they do not assert historical citizenship.
Three glass objects use the current catalogue's `unknown` work type while their
museum classification and glass medium remain preserved. Missing object types
were not invented. The initial import rolled back atomically on the unsupported
glass type; the corrected, re-pinned plan was applied successfully.

All 42 derivatives were visually reviewed on four contact sheets. Long scrolls
remain uncropped. Syrian glass is shown in the fragmentary condition recorded by
the museum. Derivatives are at most 99,539 bytes. Source originals and validated
backup are under the corresponding `Artline/source-images/event-images-20260927/`
and `Artline/backups/event-images-20260927/` Library Application Support folders.

`ops/add-event-images-20260927.py` retains the reproducible bounded workflow.
`plan.json`, `plan-pin.json`, `images.json`, `visual-review.json`, `applied.json`
and `verification.json` retain exact source records, hashes and review evidence.
Production transfer receipts are in `../production-followup-20260927/`.
