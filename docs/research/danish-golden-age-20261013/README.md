# Danish Golden Age — 13 October 2026

This pass treats the Danish Golden Age as an editorial art-historical lens with a
core period of 1801–1864. It does not treat Danish nationality alone as movement
evidence. Creator matching uses Artline's existing unique SMK person authorities;
fuzzy name matches are excluded.

The production baseline contained four movement-linked painters. The reconciled
set contains 34 creators represented by source-backed catalogue objects. The
final core reconciliation adds the exact academy authorities J.L. Lund and C.A.
Lorentzen, which were initially omitted from the closed SMK scan.
Core and associated roles are stored separately. Artline's unified-catalogue rule
makes every active work by those creators available to the lens while preserving
each work's existing date, attribution, museum, image, and review state.

The complete current SMK scan inspected all **4,579 paintings, 59,930 drawings,
and 24,643 prints** returned by the Danish-nationality catalogue scopes. It found
11,882 distinct source objects for the first 32-creator closed scan. Exact identity
reconciliation added **6,620 missing works** (6,450 drawings and 170 prints) and
excluded 169 apparent additions already represented through another production
identity path. With the two academy authorities, the resulting movement catalogue
has **12,273 active works** across 34 creators. A further 28 repeated source
accession groups and five source-URL anomalies remain held.

SMK object histories supplied 2,410 exhibition assertions. After touring-stop,
identity, and date reconciliation, production now contains **178 exhibitions,
232 occurrences in total (five curated tour occurrences plus 227 from object
histories), 2,392 exact artwork entries across 894 works, and 30 curated survey
participants plus 483 object-derived participant links** from this pass. Six
reversed source-date assertions and eleven assertions attached to three ambiguous
production accessions remain held. Future or still-running records remain
`uncertain`/`catalogued`; they are not claimed as completed displays.

The touring exhibitions are researched as occurrences of a single documented
exhibition where the source supports that relationship. SMK object-level exhibition
histories remain a separate ingestion stream: historical venue labels, source date
precision, and checklist participation must be retained, and a catalogue association
must not be promoted to physical display without explicit evidence.

Unresolved names, compound objects, qualified attributions, and source date conflicts
stay on the held checklist. This is deep source-backed catalogue coverage, not a claim
that the finite and contested art-historical canon has an objectively final membership.

## Catalogue-checklist linking — 13 October 2026

The catalogue-depth audit covers all **179** exhibitions associated with Danish
Golden Age creators or artworks. Every exhibition already had at least one entry,
but all 179 were still marked `partial`; this establishes the checklist backlog
without treating an object-history mention as a complete exhibition checklist.
The frozen audit and per-exhibition source ledger are under
`catalogue-checklists/`.

The first production catalogue wave adds **83 source-specific entries across two
exhibitions**. Getty's official seven-page checklist
for *Beyond the Light* contributes all 81 numbered entries: 51 resolve to exact
active artwork identities and 30 remain unlinked catalogue records. The official
1830 Charlottenborg catalogue was initially recorded through catalogue number
165. A complete-document correction subsequently transcribed all 193 entries,
including the omitted sculpture, architecture, ornament and applied-art sections.
The catalogue links its no. 34 to SMK KMS167 (J.C. Dahl, *Winter Landscape near
Vordingborg*) and no. 58 to SMK KMS8003 (Wilhelm Bendz, *The Waagepetersen
Family*). No title-only or approximate production link was written.

All selected checklist works in this wave were created before 1980. Existing
Artline policy still limits new catalogue artwork creation to 1970 or earlier;
therefore a 1971–1979 checklist object may be retained as exhibition evidence but
is not automatically created as a new artwork. Catalogue numbers, original title
and creator labels, lender text, source pages, capture hashes, and unresolved
identities remain in the exhibition evidence.

## Catalogue-wide reconciliation continuation

A second identity pass compared the previously unlinked catalogue rows with the
expanded production catalogue rather than only the original movement subset. It
applied **15 additional exact object links**: one Getty checklist entry and 14
entries from *Impressionist Gardens*. Each link has a unique creator/title/date
candidate corroborated by the catalogue holder, accession, and/or exact object
dimensions. Repeated-title and version collisions remain unresolved; in
particular, the private and Metropolitan Museum versions of Monet's *The Parc
Monceau* are kept distinct, and neither 1907/1908 *Water Lilies* entry is linked
to Artline's different 1906 Chicago canvas.

An accession-led continuation corrected Getty checklist no. 1 to the existing
Getty-native object and linked eight more existing museum-native objects. Six of
those Met objects now have visually reviewed official CC0 primary images. The
official page for Met 2016.678 exposes only a restricted image endpoint, so that
object is linked without an image rather than filled from an unverified source.

The three catalogue sources now contain **371 entries, 76 exact artwork links,
and 295 explicit unlinked rows**. Getty's checklist accounts for 60 links and 21
unlinked rows. Across all 179 Danish Golden Age exhibition records, production
still contains 2,868 entries; the accession pass added eight links and replaced
one duplicate-object link with its exact source-native identity.
Charlottenborg 1830 is now `complete`; the other 178 exhibitions remain `partial`
because object-history assertions do not constitute complete exhibition
checklists.

## Full document discovery and analysis round

All **179 exhibition titles** were searched against the Internet Archive corpus,
and every returned item received file-level inspection. The scan returned 34
items across 15 exhibition-title searches. Four are credible exhibition documents;
the other 30 are homonymous or contextual books, audio, video, and unrelated
records and are explicitly rejected in `document-corpus-analysis.json`.

The publicly readable 2010 *Impressionist Gardens* book was analyzed in full. Its
catalogue section contains **97 consecutive entries**, all for works made before
1980. Those 97 source entries are now in production as event-level `catalogued`
records. The initial movement-subset comparison found no exact links. The later
catalogue-wide pass linked 14 objects after holder, accession and dimensions
checks; 83 entries remain unlinked. The existing SMK object-history association
for Eckersberg's KMS2059 does not occur in the book; both pieces of evidence are
retained as a source conflict because the touring catalogue includes venue-specific
differences.

Three other exact Archive records were analyzed at metadata and file level:

- *Christen Købke: 1810–1848* (SMK, 1996), 397 pages, ISBN 8775510553;
  scan text and PDF are access restricted.
- *Christen Købke: Danish Master of Light* (2010), 128 pages, ISBN
  9780300166637; scan text and PDF are access restricted.
- *Light! The Industrial Age: 1750–1900* is a matching Carnegie Museum
  DVD-ROM supporting record, but it exposes no artwork checklist.

Official-source follow-up also identifies the 2019 *Danish Golden Age* touring
catalogue (ISBN 9788775511785) and confirms that the 2015–2016 Eckersberg
Copenhagen–Hamburg–Paris tour presented 125 works and issued a 336-page French
catalogue (ISBN 9789078655220). These documents establish publication and tour
identity; they do not establish that every work travelled to every venue.

## Pre-1980 catalogue-library continuation — 14 October 2026

The Royal Danish Library discovery API identified exact physical catalogue
records for eight of the nine remaining pre-1980 exhibitions: the 1942
Kunstforeningen jubilee, the 1954 Johan Thomas Lundbye drawings exhibition, the
1952 Ny Carlsberg Foundation jubilee, *Dansk Guldålder* (1964), both Lorenz
Frølich exhibitions (1968 and 1971), *Dansk kunst 1825–1855* (1975), and
*Fædrelandshistoriske billeder* (1978–79). Production now has a catalogue
publication record for each, with the library MMS identity, pagination, venue or
date notes, and physical holding evidence. Open Library independently corroborates
four editions with ISBN, LCCN, OCLC, publisher, or pagination metadata.

None of these eight records exposes a readable scan or checklist file. They are
therefore recorded as bibliographic evidence and access holds only; no artwork
participation was inferred from catalogue existence. *Alle tiders danske
tegninger* (1979) remains unresolved because an exact catalogue record was not
returned. The production exhibition-entry totals remain unchanged by this
bibliographic pass.
