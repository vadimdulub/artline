# Armenian and Georgian artists: source-led catalogue research

Research date: 13 September 2026. This pass combines a bounded authority survey, museum research, individual artwork selection, reproduction-rights checks, and the Women artists discovery filter. The final database and image totals are recorded in `RESULTS.md`; individual objects and image credits are listed in `INVENTORY.md`.

## What the survey establishes

The initial authority query found 709 painter identities through explicit Armenian/Georgian citizenship or ethnic-group statements. This is a discovery roster, not a definitive nationality census or an assertion that every identity is reconciled. Diaspora affiliations are retained alongside other identities. The initial local catalogue had only two profiles tagged AM/GE, but authority matching found eight relevant existing profiles with 197 linked artworks and 19 images. Country tags alone therefore substantially understated coverage.

The museum-connected discovery queries returned 12,753 distinct artwork authority IDs. Some batches reached their 1,500-row cap, so this is neither complete coverage nor an ingestion target. The first detailed sample was bounded to 350 works, balanced across artists rather than allowing a large collection to occupy the whole batch. Documented births through 1900 were a research-budget priority only: eligibility always depends on the artwork's own creation date. A second, targeted pass followed Georgian modernists, women artists and artists with qualified biographical dates. The combined detailed sample contains 443 distinct objects.

The selected record set contains 271 named-creator paintings. Non-painting types, ambiguous or qualified creators/collections, unreconciled institutions, known out-of-scope creation dates and existing global identities were separately logged. Unknown dates remain unknown. These are review records; no publication decision follows from ingestion or having an image.

## Armenian research strands

**Nineteenth-century portraiture and landscape.** The National Gallery of Armenia is the principal collection connection in this sample. Its official collection portal establishes the institutional context, while object-specific authority statements, accessions and file pages supply traceable discovery evidence. Direct automated access to the portal returned a browser-verification page; that response is preserved and was not treated as an artwork catalogue. No challenge was bypassed. The resulting collection assertions remain in review. [National Gallery collection](https://www.gallery.am/en/collection)

The selected artists include Hakob Hovnatanyan, Stepanos Nersisyan, Gevorg Bashinjaghian, Panos Terlemezyan, Stepan Aghajanian, Arshak Fetvadjian and Vardges Sureniants. The image selection includes portraiture, monastery architecture, landscapes and historical compositions. Exact object identities and individual image rights are recorded in the inventory; Armenian affiliation does not replace existing Russian, French or other documented affiliations.

**Saryan and the early twentieth century.** The Martiros Saryan House-Museum describes the National Gallery's Saryan holdings and identifies early works including *Panthers* (1907), *Under the Trees* (1907), *Persian Girl* (1910) and *Egyptian Masks* (1913). Its biographical material also explains his journeys through Constantinople, Egypt, Persia and Armenia. These sources show why geographical subject matter and the artist's cultural identity should be recorded separately. Museum web images were research references, not assumed reusable downloads. [House-Museum collection account](https://www.sarian.am/htmls_eng/other_national_gallery_of_arm_main.html), [House-Museum biography](https://www.sarian.am/htmls_eng/sarian_biography_parts.html)

**Women artists.** Mariam and Yeranuhi Aslamazyan deserve a dedicated strand rather than appearing only as incidental names in a general survey. Their museum in Gyumri documents a collection of roughly 620 donated originals across painting, graphics and ceramics. This pass researched both authorities and selected qualifying named painting records where object and collection identities could be reconciled; an artist's presence in the survey does not imply that every work was imported. Reproduction rights for these later artists remain a separate limitation. Gohar Fermanyan and Lavinia Bazhbeuk-Melikyan were also included in the research scope. [Aslamazyan Sisters Gallery](https://www.aslamazyanmuseum.com/arm/)

**Medieval Armenian illumination.** Toros Roslin is an important continuation beyond easel painting. The Walters Art Museum identifies its *T'oros Roslin Gospels*, W.539, as a manuscript made in 1262 and describes Roslin's expansion of the narrative Gospel cycle. The manuscript needs object/folio and illumination-type modelling rather than being silently imported as a painting. Its record and images are documented as a primary-source lead; this painting batch does not claim to cover Armenian medieval art. [Walters W.539](https://art.thewalters.org/object/W.539/)

## Georgian research strands

**Pirosmani.** The Georgian art archive identifies the Shalva Amiranashvili Museum collection connection for works including *Giraffe*, *Actress Margarita*, *Fisherman* and *The Childless Millionaire and Poor Woman with Children*. The Fondation Beyeler's 2023–2024 exhibition guide provides an additional primary museum context and makes clear that most exhibited works are undated. The guide explicitly dates *Five Princes Carousing* to 1906; a selected authority record currently gives 1909. That mismatch is retained as a date/identity review issue, without inventing a compromise range. Exhibition dates are not artwork creation dates and a past loan is not current display. [Georgian art archive](https://art.gov.ge/en/portfolio/niko-pirosmanashvili/), [Beyeler exhibition guide](https://www.fondationbeyeler.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/Ausstellungen/Ausstellungen_2023/web2_Saalheft_PIROSMANI_EN.pdf)

**Gigo Gabashvili and realism.** The Georgian archive connects Gabashvili's practice with academic training, Caucasus/Central Asian journeys and a substantial legacy in Georgian museum collections. Its dated examples include *The Bazaar in Samarkand* (1899) and *The Sleeping Khevsuri* (1898). His symbolic compositions broaden the picture beyond ethnographic realism. In this batch the exact identity check accepted one dated Gabashvili reproduction; a separate *Pilgrims from Samarkand* candidate was held for further file-to-object matching rather than accepted merely because the filename looked plausible. [Artist record](https://art.gov.ge/en/portfolio/gabashvili-gigo/), [2025 museum exhibition](https://art.gov.ge/en/event/realism-and-symbolism-in-gigo-gabashvilis-art/)

**Kakabadze and Gudiashvili.** These artists connect Georgian modernism with Paris and wider international collections. The archive describes Kakabadze's work in abstraction, collage and scientific invention and identifies collections outside Georgia, including Yale and the Thyssen museum. Gudiashvili's record documents paintings, illustration, theatre work and church murals. The selected catalogue retains paintings as paintings; it does not flatten sculpture, photography or wall-painting cycles into that type. [David Kakabadze](https://art.gov.ge/en/portfolio/kakabadze-david/), [Lado Gudiashvili](https://art.gov.ge/en/portfolio/gudiashvili-lado/)

**Georgian women artists.** Elene Akhvlediani is documented through the Georgian archive and encyclopedia; the latter identifies dated examples such as *Kakheti in Winter* (1924), *Corner of Paris* (1926) and *Alazani Valley* (1954). Natela Iankoshvili was included in targeted authority research. A zero-result object query is an index-coverage gap, not evidence that an artist made no eligible works. Further work should reconcile primary object records and image permissions for these artists. [Elene Akhvlediani archive](https://art.gov.ge/en/portfolio/akhvlediani-elene/), [Georgian Encyclopedia](https://georgianencyclopedia.ge/en/form_eng/372)

## Images, evidence and editorial decisions

Only selected, date-eligible works entered image preparation. Each attached reproduction has a source-linked Commons file, independent object identity confirmation, an explicit per-file reusable licence, retained credit, checksum and download receipt. The initial underlying-artwork screen conservatively deferred artists with unresolved death dates or dates after 1951; this is a workflow screen, not a universal copyright-law assertion. Museum availability, a Wikipedia illustration and a photograph's licence alone were never treated as permission for a still-protected underlying artwork.

Original selected files are retained in the session backup directory. Published-format assets preserve the source frame and proportions and are compressed to JPEGs no larger than 100,000 bytes. A contact-sheet review and checks for Commons dispute/deletion templates precede database attachment. Image failures retain the metadata record.

Museum collection statements remain review assertions unless independently validated elsewhere. The batch's owner research selections remain distinguishable from museum-designated masterpieces. No current-on-view claims were added. Existing catalogue identities, artworks, images and publication states were preserved.

## Women artists filter

The checkbox appears immediately before **Only popular painters**. It is independent of popularity; enabling both applies both predicates. URL state, reset, clear-all, reload/back navigation, counts, timeline density, suggestions, facets and painter options use the same server-side scope. Responses remain bounded.

The initial data audit examined 5,090 existing artist authority IDs and found non-deprecated P21 statements for 5,043. It selected 519 positive women-artist identities and deferred two conflicts. Preferred-rank statements take precedence. Missing evidence stays unknown; names, photographs and nationalities are never used to infer gender. Newly imported artists are checked using their captured identity statements as a separate step. [Wikidata sex or gender property](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P21)

Local and production database evidence is recorded separately. The feature implementation is validated locally; an application deployment is a separate action and was not performed during this research pass.

## Limits and next research priorities

This is a substantial selected pass, not a complete inventory of Armenian or Georgian art. Native Armenian titles are retained when no sourced English title exists. Authority-only collection claims and ambiguous dates remain reviewable, and contemporary image rights constrain coverage. Georgian objects are less well represented in the structured index than Armenian National Gallery objects; targeted museum catalogue reconciliation is the next priority. Medieval illumination, murals, anonymous traditions and non-painting media need their own explicit object models and selection passes.

Source captures, query text, capped-query indicators, initial and expanded database snapshots, identity evidence, prepared-image metadata, batch plans and application receipts are retained alongside this report. The unsuccessful first occupation query is preserved as an implementation error, not an absence-of-artists finding. Provider cooldowns and failed requests are documented; they were not circumvented.
