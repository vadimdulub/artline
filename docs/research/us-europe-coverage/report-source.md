# Artline: US and European museum coverage

Audience: Artline owner. Research and local import verified on 9 September 2026. Scope: museum discovery in the United States and Europe, with explicitly flagged boundary territories; paintings created from the atlas's existing 1100 lower bound through 1970. This is a substantial first systematic pass, not a finished census or a completed harvest of every museum.

## Executive answer

Yes: the available museum evidence is vastly larger than the original 715 artworks. This pass added 1,508 eligible National Gallery of Art paintings, bringing the working catalogue to 2,223 artworks. Separately, PostgreSQL now stores 4,542 Wikidata museum candidates, all 1,216 rows of the downloaded French Muséofile directory, the supplied 111-source registry, and all 4,455 painting records from NGA's export. These source sets overlap and must not be added together as a count of distinct museums or catalogue artworks.

Catalogue institutions remain 56: discovering a museum is not the same as validating it and adding its artworks. All artwork additions remain in review. No images were downloaded, no works published, no display claims created, and no commits made. The existing popular-painter default and personal/museum highlight selections were left unchanged.

## Louvre: your scale estimate is right

The Louvre's official site describes more than 500,000 catalogue entries across departments and the Musée national Eugène-Delacroix. This includes works on long-term loan in either direction, storage and MNR recovery records, not just paintings physically inside the Louvre. [Louvre: catalogue scope](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/page/aPropos).

The painting-department search supports more than 10,000 records. A recent search index showed 11,846, while an older cached response showed 10,559. Direct access was rate-limited during this pass, so 11,846 is not presented as a freshly recounted total. Department membership does not establish autograph authorship, physical object count or current custody. [Louvre: Paintings department](https://collections.louvre.fr/recherche?collection%5B0%5D=8&lt=list).

The official workflow supports CSV search exports and per-object ARK JSON. Ownership, holding, deposits, attribution and dates are separate fields; actual responses can have blanks. The full paintings export and its row-limit behaviour were not obtained here. Catalogue text is offered under Etalab with source/update attribution, while image reuse has separate conditions. These sources are a strong next ingestion target, not a claim that the Louvre has already been harvested. [Louvre JSON documentation](https://collections.louvre.fr/page/documentationJSON); [Louvre terms, 19 March 2026](https://collections.louvre.fr/page/cgu).

## What the museum list does and does not establish

The broad discovery layer queries Wikidata's art-museum class and subclasses, grouped by country claims. Of 58 country/territory partitions, 57 returned a usable response; 50 had at least one candidate. Italy remained unavailable after its bounded retry. Seven successful partitions had no matching records; this is a data-coverage result, not proof that they have no art museums. [Wikidata access and licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Data_access).

The resulting 4,542 unique Wikidata identities include museum organizations, buildings, branches, foundations and possible classification errors. US candidates number 940. For example, NGA and its East and West buildings appear separately. Websites are retained as source claims, not independently verified operational sites. Country boundaries, cross-country claims and historical closure information remain reviewable; the simpler retry queries did not request closure, parent or place fields.

The geographic core follows the requested US/Europe scope. Russia's whole-country candidates and Cyprus, Turkey, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia and Kosovo are explicitly flagged for geographic review rather than silently treated as uniformly inside geographic Europe. Territories are separate query partitions; the US country-claim query is not a verified 50-state/DC census. France's national directory also includes overseas museums, which remain separately flagged. Cross-source institutional deduplication is still required.

## Authoritative national directories: where to expand next

United States. IMLS's retired 2018 Museum Data Files cover the 50 states and DC. Its guide documents 2,620 ART entries using the DISCIPL field, not a present-day total of active art museums. No new establishments were added for the 2018 update. The original download was inaccessible here, and the preservation copy's download required login. The newer National Museum Survey protects institution identities, so current survey statistics are not a replacement named-museum registry. [IMLS Museum Data Files](https://www.imls.gov/research-evaluation/data-collection/museum-data-files); [2018 documentation](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/museum_data_file_documentation_and_users_guide.pdf); [NMS confidentiality](https://imls.gov/research-evaluation/surveys/national-museum-survey-nms/faqs-about-the-nms).

France. The Ministry of Culture's Muséofile is an official designation directory, not a list of every art museum. Its downloadable export yielded 1,216 records. The file uses a pipe delimiter despite its CSV extension. A conservative art-keyword pass found 798 candidates including overseas records; 786 remain in the mainland/art-candidate queue. Mixed-subject museums, non-art subjects and overseas geography are retained, not silently discarded or called art museums. The source is offered under Open Licence 2.0. [Muséofile dataset, updated 7 September 2026](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/musees-de-france-base-museofile).

Germany. The official ISIL service offers CC0 organization metadata. A one-record museum-prefix SRU probe reported 7,924 registry records, not 7,924 art museums. The working format was PicaPlus; the tested RDF conversion was unavailable. Subject interpretation, branch identity and current operation still need validation before a bulk art-museum import. [ISIL SRU documentation](https://isil.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/schnittstellen/api/sru); [linked-data licensing](https://isil.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/schnittstellen/api/linked-data-service).

United Kingdom. Mapping Museums provides a historical downloadable directory with stable IDs, subjects and opening/closure intervals. It includes closed and unaccredited museums. Its September 2021 export cannot establish today's operating status; the application changed in March 2026, and a current bulk export was not verified. Wales offers a current official accreditation list, but repeated IDs and working-towards sections require reconciliation. [Mapping Museums data](https://museweb.dcs.bbk.ac.uk/data); [2026 application change](https://mapping-museums.bbk.ac.uk/2026/03/03/major-changes-to-the-mapping-museums-lab-database/); [Welsh Government list, 15 May 2026](https://www.gov.wales/accredited-museums-wales-html).

Nordic countries. Finland has 2025 operations/site CSVs and an art-museum-site workbook, with CC BY 4.0 statistical-data terms, but a professional-museum scope and exclusions including Åland. Denmark's official directory excludes museums outside its state/recognized system. Sweden's statistics distinguish staffing thresholds and smaller museums; Norway's national-network figures cover a funding/organizational subset. These are useful coverage checks, not interchangeable all-museum lists. [Finland data](https://www.museotilasto.fi/statpublication/year/2025), [licence and scope](https://www.museotilasto.fi/info); [Denmark directory](https://slks.dk/omraader/kulturinstitutioner/museer/om-statsanerkendte-og-statslige-museer); [Sweden 2025](https://kulturanalys.se/publikation/museer-2025/); [Norway statistics](https://www.kulturdirektoratet.no/fag/museum-og-kulturarv/museumsutvikling/museumsstatistikk).

Other European routes. Italy's cultural-places graph mixes museum and non-museum entities; RDF triple counts are not museum counts, and national download/licence verification remains incomplete. Spain combines museums and museographic collections under regional definitions. Netherlands and Austria provide quality registers rather than every museum; Ireland's programme includes inactive sites and Northern Ireland. The research investigated these source routes but did not ingest their full directories. Switzerland, Belgium and much of Eastern/Southeastern Europe's national directories remain an evidence gap beyond the Wikidata discovery layer. [Italy cultural places](https://dati.cultura.gov.it/lodview/resource/datasetLuoghiDellaCultura.html); [Spain directory](https://aplicaciones.cultura.gob.es/gesdirmuseos/eldirectorio.jsp); [Netherlands register](https://www.museumregisternederland.nl/register-bekijken); [Austria registration](https://www.museumsbund.at/museumspraxis/museumsregistrierung-in-oesterreich); [Ireland programme](https://www.heritagecouncil.ie/museum-standards-programme-participants).

## NGA: a reproducible catalogue import

The pinned 9 September NGA export contains 145,707 objects, including 4,455 records classified as Painting. The six captured metadata tables preserve identities, dates, material, labelled dimensions, inscriptions, provenance, acquisition credits, creator/owner relationships, terms, object-part relationships and catalogue texts. Local counts were calculated from the downloaded files; they are not website estimates. Image files and the image metadata dump were not fetched. [NGA immutable export](https://github.com/NationalGalleryOfArt/opendata/commit/f088836026d09d0d25001814fba0f84d757ebe62).

The source documentation exposes an important trap: when a creation-date label is blank, numeric date bounds may be the artist's lifetime. This importer requires a meaningful creation label consistent with the numerical bounds. It also checks accessioned/physical status, current creator relationships and exact authority IDs. Virtual covers and inseparable child records are deferred; separable physical panels are not discarded merely because they have a parent. [NGA Data Dictionary](https://github.com/NationalGalleryOfArt/opendata/blob/f088836026d09d0d25001814fba0f84d757ebe62/documentation/Data%20Dictionary.txt).

A total of 1,510 records passed every gate and matched 356 existing painter authorities. Two were already present; 1,508 are new. The NGA catalogue now includes 27 Monet works, 17 Pissarro works, 23 Cézanne works and 12 Rembrandt works. These are sourced museum holdings, not newly invented museum-highlight designations. The original 1,000-painter cohort is an existing identity constraint of this pass, not a museum-collection limit or a limit on Artline's future scope.

The main local expansion opportunity is 1,699 otherwise eligible records awaiting artist-authority reconciliation. Of these, 1,636 carry source-supplied Wikidata IDs representing 647 distinct IDs outside the current matched cohort; 63 have no usable QID. These are not necessarily unknown creators at the museum. They should be reconciled and added as sourced review artists in a subsequent batch, checking name/authority conflicts and date evidence rather than creating duplicate painters.

Metadata CC0 does not make every linked reproduction unrestricted. Image review remains a separate workflow. [NGA open-access policy](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/free-images-and-open-access).

## Implementation and verification

Go and PostgreSQL own the import and review logic. Discovery/source evidence is stored separately from curated institutions and artworks, using immutable checksum-addressed snapshots, exact source identities, raw JSON and indexed review/country keys. Imports are local-only and default to rollback previews. Replays do not add duplicates or restore fields that an editor has cleared. Next.js receives its existing bounded API responses; no full catalogue is loaded into the browser.

The full Go suite with isolated PostgreSQL fixtures and go vet pass. A 100,000-row unrelated-data identity test used the expected indexes without a sequential scan; this is not a 10-million-row benchmark. The new review-page query used its index and returned 50 rows in about 2.4 ms on the local evidence store. The live NGA endpoint returns 1,510 works, zero current-display assertions and a five-item cursor page. Catalogue and evidence replays left the measured counts unchanged.

A fresh PostgreSQL public-schema backup was taken before changes and its archive contents verified; a full restore was not tested. All 1,508 new artworks passed direct review-status, date, media and publication checks. Existing titles, dates, cleared values, institutions and curated choices were preserved. Rich source provenance and catalogue text are currently available in the backend evidence store, not as a new reader-facing provenance/article interface.

First, reconcile the already downloaded NGA artist-authority backlog: this can unlock more sourced paintings without new object requests. Then obtain the Louvre's official painting search export and validate its identity/date/deposit fields before requesting object JSON in bounded batches. Current access failures are recorded; no rate limit or login was bypassed.

Rijksmuseum's current documentation supports no-key OAI-PMH, curated sets, resumption tokens and incremental harvesting. Prefer this documented route to old API-key examples. Its multi-domain archive page includes library records and image metadata, so archive file counts must not be called painting counts. [Rijksmuseum OAI-PMH](https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/docs/oai-pmh/); [current data dumps](https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/docs/data-dumps/).

For the US, the Met provides CC0 metadata for over 470,000 objects in its official Git LFS CSV, and Chicago recommends its dumps for bulk use. Both need painting/date/creator/holdings filters and separate image-rights checks. These are researched next sources, not newly completed imports. [Met open data](https://github.com/metmuseum/openaccess); [Chicago documentation](https://api.artic.edu/docs/).

Completing the requested all-museum scope still requires national-directory reconciliation, institution-versus-venue crosswalks, geographically precise coverage, many source-specific adapters and refresh/review workflows. This pass establishes the searchable evidence and a substantially larger catalogue; it does not claim to have visited every museum website or collected every catalogue. Research stopped after primary support for the operational import, tested access routes, documented national-directory distinctions and bounded failures; remaining sources are explicitly queued rather than reported as complete.
