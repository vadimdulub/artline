# World War II: Asia and Africa

Researched companion to **The Second World War**, 31 October 2026. The original topic and its selection remain intact. The companion uses eight existing illustrated artworks, four existing books and eighteen existing events. Its main period is 1931–1945; background and aftermath extend from 1930 to 1970. This is a first curated selection with documented gaps, not a complete account of Asian or African experiences.

## Why change the starting point?

A chronology beginning only in September 1939 obscures Japan’s earlier expansion in China. The Hong Kong Museum of the War of Resistance and Coastal Defence identifies the 1931 Mukden Incident and the 1937 Marco Polo Bridge Incident as different stages. The companion therefore starts in 1931, while retaining 1937–1945 for the existing full-scale Sino-Japanese War event. The museum page was read successfully; a later web-reader timeout is recorded separately and was not retried. [Museum exhibition](https://hk.waranddefence.museum/en/web/mcd/pictorial-exhibition.html).

The question is also whose testimony is visible. A Japanese official war painting, a Chinese resistance image, a South African labour picture, and a later survivor narrative have different purposes and relationships to events. Neither an artist’s nationality nor a wartime production date establishes political affiliation or a war subject.

## China: occupation, resistance and competing pictures

Xu Beihong’s **Put Down Your Whip** (1939) supplies the cover. It depicts performer Wang Ying and connects to anti-invasion street theatre and fundraising in Singapore. It must remain distinct from Situ Qiao’s 1940 painting with the same English title. The existing catalogue identity and image were checked; Sotheby’s historical lot description and Lo-fen I’s indexed scholarly abstract provided corroborating discovery evidence. The latter’s full PDF returned 404, so no full-paper reading is claimed. [Sotheby’s lot](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2007/contemporary-chinese-art-i-hk0249/lot.29.html), [indexed paper location](https://humanities.ccu.edu.tw/var/file/56/1056/img/1319/16_05.pdf).

Lao She’s **Four Generations under One Roof** brings occupied Beijing into the book sequence. Its stored 1944 date marks the beginning of publication, not completion of every part of the novel. The existing book’s sourced overview and date basis are preserved.

Chinese resistance should eventually include more than one famous painter. The University of Chicago’s exhibition with the Chinese University of Hong Kong documents wartime Yan’an woodcuts by Gu Yuan, Yan Han and Li Qun. Existing Artline records for Gu Yuan’s **The Eighth Route Army in Learning** and Yan Han’s **Peasants Defend Their Grain** are strong candidates. They currently lack eligible pictures; they are research targets, not illustrated additions. Cleveland’s existing source holds remain binding. [University exhibition](https://uchicago.hk/exhibitions/art-war-chinese-woodcuts-yanan-ca-1944).

## Japan: imperial representation and civilian memory

Foujita’s **Battle on the Bank of the Halha** was painted in 1941 about the 1939 Khalkhin Gol fighting. **Suicidal Attu Island** is the existing catalogue title for the 1943 composition institutionally called **Final Fighting on Attu**. Both enter as Japanese official war painting requiring critical reading. The selection does not present imperial propaganda as a neutral report or endorse its heroic framing. The official Japanese collection record was available through indexed metadata; held museum delivery routes were not reopened. [Collection record](https://search.artmuseums.go.jp/records.php?sakuhin=11437).

Civilian suffering must not erase Japanese aggression, and propaganda must not stand in for every Japanese experience. The Maruki Gallery’s account of Iri and Toshi Maruki’s **Hiroshima Panels** is an important counterpoint: the series began in 1950 and developed through later testimony and remembrance. It is not a single painting made in August 1945. No image or fabricated catalogue record was added for the panels. [Maruki Gallery](https://marukigallery.jp/en/hiroshimapanels/).

Masuji Ibuse’s **Black Rain** enters as later literary memory. Artline’s existing 1965 inception/composition date and its literal date basis are retained; indexed scholarship separately distinguishes 1965–1966 serialization from the 1966 book. The full scholarly PDF returned a web-reader 429 and was not retried. John Hersey’s **Hiroshima** (1946) is explicitly an American reporter’s account of survivors. Neither author’s book is silently treated as unmediated survivor autobiography. [Indexed chronology reference](https://reitaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/183/files/023-056_Lin.pdf).

Yasuo Kuniyoshi offers another future thread: Japanese identity in the United States, enemy-alien restrictions and opposition to Japanese militarism. Smithsonian’s exhibition documents these tensions. Generic portraits and circus pictures were not selected merely because he made them during the war. [Smithsonian exhibition](https://americanart.si.edu/exhibitions/kuniyoshi/online/wwii-years).

## Africa: resistance, military service and unequal civilian life

Ethiopia belongs near the beginning. Seltene Seyoum’s research, published by the Journal of Ethiopian Studies and hosted by Addis Ababa University, distinguishes Italian conquest from continuing Ethiopian resistance in 1936–1941. This provides an Ethiopian research perspective alongside British institutional campaign histories. The article’s abstract was read; the full article was not downloaded. The existing Second Italo-Ethiopian War record retains its stored 1935–1937 range, which is not used as an end date for all resistance. [Journal abstract](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/JES/article/view/9063).

The existing Ethiopian painting **The Battle of Gonder and the Surrender of the Italians to the British in Ethiopia**, circa 1942, is the strongest currently identified African-made battle image. It remains an unpictured research target because its provider is held. No substitute European painting is labelled as an Ethiopian voice.

South Africa exposes the contradiction between Allied service and racial inequality. J. S. Mohlamme’s historical study documents unequal roles, pay and demobilization, while acknowledging exceptions to the official restrictions on bearing arms. The story does not generalize South African military rules to all African forces. [Study hosted by South African History Online](https://sahistory.org.za/archive/soldiers-without-reward-africans-south-africas-wars).

Gerard Sekoto supplies three existing pictures of civilian and labour relations: **Yellow Houses: A Street in Sophiatown** (1940), **Convicts Cutting a Hedge** (1944), and **The Song of the Pick** (1946–1947). These are labelled social context. Their presence does not assert that WWII caused a specific imprisonment or that the paintings depict military labour. Imbali identifies the last work’s basis in Andrew Goldie’s 1930s photograph; its postwar date stays visible. [Imbali Artbooks](https://imbaliartbooks.org.za/gerard-sekoto/).

Ferdinand Oyono’s **The Old Man and the Medal** (1956) adds a Cameroonian literary critique of colonial recognition and sacrifice. The African Union library’s English edition is dated 1969; that edition date does not replace the catalogue’s original-publication date. [African Union Commission Library](https://library.au.int/old-man-and-medal-3).

## Connected theatres: Burma, India, the Philippines and Indonesia

Burma connects the regional histories: the National Army Museum documents Indian, Chinese and African forces alongside British forces. Its interpretation is identified as British institutional history, not a replacement for all participants’ testimony. Singapore, Manila, the Bengal famine and Quit India are separate selected events; they are not interchangeable examples of a single Asian experience. [Far East campaign](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/far-east-campaign).

Nandalal Bose’s **Gandhi March (Bapuji)** dates to 1930 and supplies an anti-colonial prelude. It does not depict Quit India in 1942. Gandhi’s own letter of 3 August 1942 connects demands for independence with resistance to Japanese aggression, challenging a simple equation of anti-British politics with support for the Axis. [Gandhi’s letter](https://www.mkgandhi.org/letters/unstates/letter_amer_friends.php).

Indonesia’s 1945 proclamation and the subsequent national revolution remain distinct events. Sudjojono’s **Kami Present, Ibu Pertiwi** dates to 1965 and enters as later national memory. No exact WWII battle is assigned to it. The existing restricted image explicitly identifies the museum-supplied reproduction as cropped; its supplied bytes and credit remain unchanged. [National Gallery Singapore collection metadata](https://www.nationalgallery.sg/sg/en/our-collections/search-collection.artwork.html/national-collection/s--sudjojono/2011/2011-02252%20%28cropped%29.jpg.html).

Existing Amorsolo records for **Burning of Intendencia** and **Bombing of Intendencia** are further Philippine targets. They have different titles, collection identities and dimensions and must not be merged. No picture was imported or licence inferred from a search thumbnail.

## Selection and picture audit

The exact eight accepted UUIDs, original media metadata, unchanged rights labels and individual reading cues are in [ARTWORKS.md](ARTWORKS.md). All nine candidate frames were actually viewed on one contact sheet. Foujita’s **Forcing into Hankou** was excluded because the current monochrome reproduction carries a visible PacificWrecks.org watermark and its reproduction status remains ambiguous. The original record is untouched.

Five already successful application images were reused from the parent cache; four further images were read from Artline’s existing public asset URLs. All nine matched their database SHA-256 checksums. There were **zero new provider-image downloads, uploads, artwork/profile inserts, rights changes or claim writes**. Search engines supplied discovery and corroboration, not image permissions. Existing fair-use/restricted labels remain literal; this research does not upgrade them to open licences.

The eight accepted works all passed the existing public gallery’s date, study and image-only filters before the new preset was created. Three are directly related wartime works; five have explicit background, civilian-context or aftermath connections. The initial background-and-aftermath window shows all eight works; switching to the main period shows five. Earlier and later books similarly remain in their actual date windows.

Primary gaps remain Ethiopian battle art, Chinese resistance prints, Philippine occupation imagery, Japanese civilian art, Bengal famine art, and North/West African visual testimony. These are explicit research gaps, not completed coverage or implicit permission to reopen held providers. No additional image recovery or automatic research job has been started.

The new configuration is appended to [the Atlas presets](../../../apps/server/internal/atlas/presets.json). Raw JSON selection, visual and release receipts remain local under the repository’s research-evidence policy. Browser visual review remains unavailable in this session; image inspection, tests and HTTP checks are recorded separately.
