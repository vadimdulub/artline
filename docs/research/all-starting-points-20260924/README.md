# All-tab historical starting-point review — 24 September 2026

This review covers all **30 starting points**, their default countries/creators/Highlights settings, and their explicitly linked event sequences. It does not claim to validate every event or artwork record in the catalogue. Selections are interpretive introductions supported by the linked institutions, not assertions that every work by a selected artist depicts the event.

## Result and selection rules

- French Revolution starts with **France**, six connected artists and political writing; Highlights is off.
- US civil rights starts with **United States**, **16 Black artists** and five Black writers; Highlights is off. Thomas Weeks Barrett is not selected. Pippin and some works are explicitly earlier context; Andrews’s 1969 activism is aftermath.
- Eleven specific movements/events use researched creator identities and explicit book selections. Broad cultural surveys use countries and, where appropriate, Highlights. Book selection avoids unrelated fiction by an otherwise relevant author.
- Every event lane uses a reviewed list of core and contextual event IDs. Five newly selected existing records add the Terror, February/October 1917, and Vostok 1/6. No event records were inserted or published.
- Countries default to the artwork layer, preserving relevant international writing and historical events. The user can apply countries to all layers. Historical state labels are retained separately; these filters do not reconstruct historical borders.
- Byzantium retains country **or recorded object tradition** matching. Hard creator/country defaults would exclude anonymous icons and mosaics without geographic links, including works held abroad.
- Selecting a different starting point replaces its countries, creators and Highlights setting together. Explicit custom filters and deliberately cleared selections survive reloads. More than three selected creators/countries collapse into a count chip; individual choices remain in the picker.
- Artwork eligibility, visibility, existing image rules, date bounds and the 1970 artwork cutoff are unchanged. Unknown dates remain unplaced. Broad or conflicting catalogue dates were not invented or silently rewritten.

## Decisions for every starting point

### Writing and the first cities

Select Mesopotamian and Egyptian urban traditions alongside the Indus, China and Mesoamerica. Anonymous objects remain eligible. The reviewed events cover Uruk, early Egypt, the Indus and Hammurabi; the Neolithic is precursor context. No arbitrary ancient rulers are added.

**Artwork countries:** iraq, iran, syria, turkey, egypt, pakistan, india, china, mexico, mesopotamia, sumer, babylonia, ancient egypt.

**Core event selection:** Uruk period; Early Dynastic Egypt; Indus Valley Civilisation; Code of Hammurabi.

**Context events:** Neolithic Revolution; Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Period references:** [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-origins-of-writing).

### The classical world

Retain the Mediterranean, Persian and connected western Asian worlds, with Maurya and Byzantine context. Use museum-supported objects rather than a named-artist canon; ancient and modern geographic labels remain distinct.

**Artwork countries:** greece, italy, turkey, cyprus, egypt, tunisia, libya, israel, lebanon, syria, iran, iraq, france, spain, portugal, united kingdom, ancient greece, ancient rome, roman empire, byzantine empire, achaemenid empire, medo-persia.

**Core event selection:** Roman Empire; Achaemenid Empire; Greco-Persian Wars; Peloponnesian War; Wars of Alexander the Great; Edict of Milan.

**Context events:** Maurya Empire; Byzantine Empire.

**Period references:** [The Met — Greek and Roman art](https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/the-year-one-art-of-the-ancient-world-east-and-west).

### Buddhism and its early journeys

Follow transmission from South Asia to Central, East and Southeast Asia. Keep anonymous devotional objects and the Dhammapada. The First Buddhist Council has unknown local dates and must remain unplaced; Maurya, Han and Tang supply dated context.

**Artwork countries:** india, nepal, pakistan, afghanistan, sri lanka, china, japan, south korea, north korea, korea, cambodia, thailand, myanmar, indonesia, vietnam, laos, tibet, gupta empire, maurya empire, tang dynasty, han dynasty, chinese empire.

**Core event selection:** First Buddhist council; Maurya Empire.

**Context events:** Tang dynasty; Han dynasty.

**Period references:** [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/buddhism-and-buddhist-art).

### The Silk Roads

Follow interconnected Eurasian and Mediterranean regions, not one modern nationality. Han, Tang, Song, Mongol and Byzantine periods establish exchange context; conquest and plague are explicitly contextual. Country matching is an introductory survey, not evidence that each object travelled the routes.

**Artwork countries:** china, mongolia, kazakhstan, uzbekistan, tajikistan, kyrgyzstan, turkmenistan, afghanistan, india, pakistan, iran, iraq, syria, turkey, greece, italy, egypt, georgia, armenia, azerbaijan, chinese empire, han dynasty, tang dynasty, mongol empire, byzantine empire, abbasid caliphate, yuan dynasty.

**Core event selection:** Han dynasty; Tang dynasty; Northern Song dynasty; Mongol Empire; Byzantine Empire.

**Context events:** Early Muslim conquests; Black Death.

**Period references:** [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2012/byzantium-and-islam/blog/topical-essays/posts/commerce).
