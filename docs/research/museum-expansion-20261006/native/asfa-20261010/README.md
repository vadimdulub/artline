All 258 individual records were checked against their linked [native gallery pages](https://exhibition.asktdigital.gr/). This remains a bounded selection: 3741 of 4012 source entries lie outside the reviewed index and additional comparison. The institution's separate description of over 9000 physical works is not an eligible artwork count.

All source thumbnails, 148 selected native originals, eleven thumbnail contacts, seven original contacts and six prepared contacts were inspected. Three pairs of prints were confirmed as separate impressions using full inventory labels, pencil signatures, paper condition and inking. The two portraits with labels 00752 and 00752_ZOG727 show different people and compositions; their full labels remain intact. Classroom studies of the same model were distinguished by artist, view and execution. Copies of religious, historical and decorative works retain their own dates and makers rather than adopting dates from the prototypes.

Bounded production reconciliation compared 197 artworks and 278 citations and preserved full snapshots. Nine further WikiArt image comparisons found two exact existing works: Mathiopoulos's *Portait of John Polemis* and Nikolaou's *Drawing of a man*. They received holding links rather than duplicate records. The Polemis primary image remains unchanged, with the complete native photograph added as an alternate. Nikolaou received a primary image; its existing 1929 catalogue date remains unchanged while the gallery's 1930 claim is recorded in evidence. All other old metadata, creator links, images and statuses remain preserved.

Ten new creator links use verified existing Moralis and Mathiopoulos records. Their Greek labels and linked person authorities agree with the existing identities and lifespans. Nikolaou's existing link is preserved. Other named creators remain source labels without invented biographies. The label “Orphan” is not treated as a person, and sitter authorities are not treated as makers.

Six new creation dates remain unresolved. Five Kefallinos prints carry source dates of 1953, 1954 or 1954–1957 but relate to the [portfolio published in 1956](https://www.culture.gov.gr/el/information/SitePages/view.aspx?nID=2455); the specific impression or proof has not been established. No 1956 year was invented for those objects. A work attributed to Theofilos is dated 1958, after his death; its date and creator role remain qualified. Another explicitly titled copy preserves its 1958 date with an unidentified copyist after Theofilos. These uncertainties are visible in review records.
