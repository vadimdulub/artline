# Guildhall additions — 9 October 2026

Added **22 source-backed artworks in review** to Guildhall Art Gallery. The catalogue now has **115 linked records**, up from 93, and **86 date-eligible records**, up from 64. The preferred 200 target and 100 date-eligible minimum remain unfinished.

Reviewed 40 selected object leads: 22 additions, 17 held and one already catalogued Lavery. Repeated Google Arts & Culture presentations, detail images and infrared documentation do not create extra artworks. Twenty-one additions retain unknown accession numbers; only Collier’s 1882 *Clytemnestra* has a supported inventory 577. An asset ID is not an accession number.

Sources include [Guildhall’s published collection](https://artsandculture.google.com/partner/guildhall-art-gallery), its [official collection history](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/things-to-do/attractions-museums-entertainment/guildhall-art-gallery/collections/collections-history), and the City of London’s [Faith collection story](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/faith-city-of-london-corporation/TAVBbPVYBd2CJg?hl=en). Four selected paintings from that story are Goodall’s 1860 *Early Morning in the Wilderness of Shur*, Phillip’s 1864 *Faith*, Armitage’s 1868 *Herod’s Birthday Feast* and Fildes’s 1914 *Naomi*. Their exact object pages were captured before review. Rights labels and original source wording remain in the evidence; no image was downloaded or attached.

The Guildhall 1882 *Clytemnestra* is distinct from the Worcester c. 1914 painting already catalogued as FAO3. [Archival research on both paintings](https://journals.akademicka.pl/saac/article/download/6303/5843/8485) supplies separate inventories, dimensions and compositional differences. Guildhall’s full-size Constable oil sketch is identified by the explicit Guildhall lender credit in [Dordrecht’s historical exhibition story](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/in-the-light-of-cuyp-dordrechts-museum/2QXRF_i5lV-Lcw?hl=en), linked to the exact object asset; the 1829 asset date and Guildhall history’s 1829–31 range are both preserved. It is distinct from Tate’s small N01814 study. Dordrecht publishing the asset does not make the painting a Dordrecht holding or establish current display.

Hunt’s 1848 finished *Eve of Saint Agnes* and its oil study have different described compositions and count as separate physical paintings. The source’s Millais contribution to the finished painting remains in evidence. The anonymous bridge-demolition work preserves the qualified signature reading J.W.S. Griffier’s 1739/1739–40 date variation remains explicit. Stanley’s 1969 *Smithfield Market* and Howard’s 1962 *Billingsgate Market* meet the creation cutoff.
