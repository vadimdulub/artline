# European painting collections: deeper expansion

Research and local import completed 9 September 2026. Artline personal study catalogue. This is a substantial selected expansion, not a census of every European museum or a claim to have captured everything each museum knows.

## Result

The reviewed batch covers 157 paintings, 41 existing painters and 28 museum collections in 14 countries. The local database gained 155 artworks, 19 institutions and 338 citations. Two existing National Gallery records were matched by accession and enriched without duplicating them. Seventeen museum-designated highlights were added: ten from the Louvre's own masterpiece album and seven from the National Gallery of Ireland's highlights. These are not additions to the owner's personal must-see lists.

Artline now contains 600 artworks and 55 institutions, with the existing 1,001 active painters and 211 media assets unchanged. All new records remain in review. No publication, new image downloads, current-display assertions, commits, infrastructure changes or deployment occurred. The 1970 boundary applies to artwork creation, not artist lifespans or museum acquisition dates. One month-qualified date remains numerically unknown instead of being forced into an inaccurate year interval.

## Coverage and selection

Coverage spans Austria, Belgium, Denmark, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland and the United Kingdom. New collection branches include Mauritshuis, Van Gogh Museum, Kröller-Müller, Brera, Borghese, Gulbenkian, SMK, Groeningemuseum, Kunsthaus Zürich, the Courtauld and regional Spanish museums. Pitti is separate from the Uffizi building, and the Alte and Neue Pinakothek remain separate collections even when works are displayed in another branch.

Three bounded research lanes covered Iberia/Italy, Northern/Central Europe, and France/UK/Ireland. Selection prioritised exact official painting records and the existing painter authorities. The National Gallery collector inspected 227 bounded metadata candidates across 18 fixed artist queries and selected 52; 175 exclusion/cap decisions were retained. Four strict source-name/attribution patterns produced no automatic selection, not evidence that the museum lacks those artists. The Louvre batch is eleven exact objects: ten linked on the first page of its masterpiece album plus the separately researched Lacemaker. The album contains forty objects across media; this import does not claim all forty or the complete Louvre painting collection. [National Gallery API documentation](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/documentation/ngacuk/collection-data/elasticsearch-api), [Louvre masterpiece album](https://collections.louvre.fr/album/2).

The museum-level appendix states source access limitations. Some objects were verified from richly indexed official technical sheets when direct retrieval was blocked. Such evidence is explicitly distinguished from successful direct API/HTML retrieval. Search hits describing copies, prints, archives or temporary loans were not accepted as original painting holdings. No access controls were bypassed, credentials requested or institutions contacted.

## What richer records now preserve

Each imported candidate carries an exact source link, original display date and reviewed year precision, institution identity, available accession/object identifiers, factual material/support and unframed dimensions, plus publisher, retrieval date and known source update date. Where the source omits the paint binder, only the support is described; it is not silently converted to oil paint. Aliases, acquisition notes, ownership/loan distinctions, attribution qualifiers and conflicts remain in source-backed notes and the raw import evidence. The database's ownership model has not been expanded: owner names remain evidence text, while custody uses the existing structured collection/loan context.

National Gallery production-place fields populate missing creation-place text when explicitly supplied. A painting's depicted scene or museum city is not used to guess where it was painted. Louvre dimensions exclude frame/accessory measurements. API ingestion/processing timestamps are not substituted for curatorial update dates. Zürich modification timestamps are retained with their unspecified timezone; historical provenance research-state dates are separately noted. Source narratives were not rewritten as original biographies or copied wholesale into artwork descriptions.

## Important evidence decisions

Museum designation is now grounded in a specific museum-authored page. The Louvre's ten selected paintings and Ireland's seven selected highlights get institution-curated selections with citations and retrieval dates. Other famous paintings are not automatically labelled masterpieces merely because they appear in a museum database. [Louvre album](https://collections.louvre.fr/album/2), [Ireland highlights](https://www.nationalgallery.ie/art-and-artists/highlights-collection).

Caravaggio's Taking of Christ is held in Dublin on indefinite loan from the Jesuit Community; its holder is not recorded as its owner. The Van Gogh Museum paintings are Foundation-owned permanent loans. Friedrich's Wanderer belongs to Stiftung Hamburger Kunstsammlungen and is on permanent loan to Hamburger Kunsthalle. Zürich's Cliff at Dieppe belongs to Vereinigung Zürcher Kunstfreunde, whereas Parliament became a donation in 1995 and the six-metre Water Lily Pond was a gift in 1952, not a Bührle Foundation loan. [Taking of Christ](https://www.nationalgallery.ie/art-and-artists/highlights-collection/taking-christ-michelangelo-merisi-da-caravaggio), [Van Gogh Sunflowers](https://www.vangoghmuseum.nl/en/collection/s0031V1962), [Wanderer](https://online-sammlung.hamburger-kunsthalle.de/en/objekt/HK-5161/wanderer-ueber-dem-nebelmeer), [Zürich Cliff](https://collection.kunsthaus.ch/en/collection/item/2848/).

Raphael's Thyssen Portrait of a Young Man retains the museum's Raphael-and-collaborator attribution. Gulbenkian's Pallas Athena retains Rembrandt-and-pupil authorship. The named painter remains credited, but the note does not assert sole authorship or invent the unnamed collaborator's identity. Historical misattributions, such as Ribera or Honthorst for paintings now catalogued as Caravaggio, are not promoted to current artist relationships. [Thyssen portrait](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/raphael-and-collaborator/portrait-young-man), [Pallas Athena](https://gulbenkian.pt/museu/en/works/pallas-athena/).

Borghese's David with the Head of Goliath has alternative date proposals, 1606–07 or 1609–1610. Its 1606–1610 numeric envelope is for indexing, not a claim that painting continued throughout that interval. Giorgione's Tempest retains the preferred around-1504 proposal and wider 1503–1509 envelope; contradictory acquisition prose is not resolved by choosing a convenient year. Raphael's Madonna of the Goldfinch says before February 1506. Artline's year-only before rule is exclusive, so treating it as before the year 1506 would incorrectly exclude January 1506. The literal date is retained and numeric years are left null for review. [Borghese David](https://www.collezionegalleriaborghese.it/en/opere/david-with-the-head-of-goliath), [Tempest](https://www.gallerieaccademia.it/opera/la-tempesta/), [Goldfinch](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/mary-christ-and-the-young-john-the-baptist-known-as-the-madonna-of-the-goldfinch).

Current object dates take precedence over weaker generic page text without erasing the disagreement: the Courtauld Bar at the Folies-Bergère says 1882, while an older virtual tour said 1883; the Van Gogh Museum Grey Felt Hat header says September–October 1887 while its meta-description still says winter 1887–88. Stockholm's Rembrandt Self-Portrait has a signed-1630 label AND museum prose explicitly dating the painting to 1630; this additional evidence distinguishes it from the previously imported Monet whose signature-only date remains unknown. [Courtauld object](https://gallerycollections.courtauld.ac.uk/object-p-1934-sc-234), [Grey Felt Hat](https://www.vangoghmuseum.nl/en/collection/s0016V1962), [Stockholm Self-Portrait](https://collection.nationalmuseum.se/en/collection/item/22374/).

## Data routes and image rights

The National Gallery's documented Elasticsearch API and Louvre's documented per-object JSON endpoints were successfully used for cached, bounded factual snapshots. The former distinguishes CC0 structured metadata, CC BY narrative and CC BY-NC-ND images. Louvre text is under the Etalab Open Licence with source/update attribution; photograph use is separately conditioned. These are practical metadata-adapter candidates, not permission for indiscriminate image crawling. [NG licences](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/documentation/ngacuk/licences), [Louvre JSON documentation](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/page/documentationJSON), [Louvre terms](https://collections.louvre.fr/page/cgu).

SMK's documented core REST API was successfully checked for six selected paintings, all with public_domain=true. Its separately generated AI enrichment is not treated as curator-verified description. Bruges' two Van Eyck IIIF manifests work and explicitly state Public Domain Mark 1.0, but their linked LIDO/OAI export returns HTTP 401: that advertised route is not operational for this client. Zürich's page-embedded data and PDFs are useful but not a verified public bulk API. [SMK API](https://api.smk.dk/api/v1/docs), [SMK data explanation](https://www.smk.dk/article/dataforklaring/), [Bruges Madonna manifest](https://dam.museabrugge.be/iiif/3/6726/manifest.json), [Bruges Margareta manifest](https://dam.museabrugge.be/iiif/3/414/manifest.json).

Belvedere illustrates why licences must be checked per reproduction. The current Kiss photograph is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; Judith is limited to private/scientific use without separate permission. Their operational IIIF manifests expose null licence fields, which do not override webpage restrictions. Nationalmuseum also mixes PD and CC BY-SA photographs on the same object. Selected Pinakothek records explicitly carry CC BY-SA 4.0, subject to attribution and share-alike requirements. No files were downloaded under any of these leads in this batch. [The Kiss](https://sammlung.belvedere.at/objects/6678/der-kuss-liebespaar), [Judith](https://sammlung.belvedere.at/objects/3492/judith), [Nationalmuseum rights](https://www.nationalmuseum.se/en/explore-art-and-design/images/rights-and-reproductions), [Pinakothek usage](https://www.sammlung.pinakothek.de/de/usage).

Italy's ICCD documents linked-data, SPARQL/REST and OAI-PMH routes, with CC BY 4.0 catalogue metadata. These merit a future authority-aware adapter, but this review did not resolve all selected Italian paintings through those identifiers. A critical rejected match demonstrates the risk: national catalogue object 1500027745 discusses Caravaggio in narrative but directly credits Andrea Vaccaro and locates the work in a church. It was not attached to Capodimonte's Caravaggio Flagellation. [ICCD interoperability](https://iccd.cultura.gov.it/it/per-condividere/interoperabilita), [ICCD open data](https://iccd.cultura.gov.it/it/per-condividere/dati-aperti), [Rejected object](https://www.catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/1500027745).

## Remaining gaps and next useful work

Capodimonte's Flagellation is a strong separately documented holding lead, with Fondo Edifici di Culto ownership and museum custody, but no exact accession/object identifier was resolved. It remains outside this import. The museum's guest Ecce Homo is not inferred to belong to Capodimonte. [Capodimonte holding evidence](https://capodimonte.cultura.gov.it/la-flagellazione-di-caravaggio-rientra-al-museo-di-capodimonte/).

Dresden, Ateneum/Finland, Basel and Bern still need exact-object follow-up. Czechia, Hungary and Poland were not pursued in this bounded expansion. Orsay has further exact-object leads, but no additional Orsay records were imported here. MNAA follow-up was blocked; Venice direct requests returned 403, Bilbao/Seville relied partly on official indexed sheets, and Uffizi's inventory portal timed out. These are access/verification gaps, not claims of empty collections or absent APIs. The source inventory and exclusion notes make them explicit.

Priorities are incremental Go adapters for the operational NG/Louvre/SMK routes; a reviewed image-selection queue carrying exact asset licence/credit; resolution of remaining authority and object-version gaps; and field-level conflict review before publication. Wider source registry reconciliation, full holdings pagination, a validated genre taxonomy, acquisition/provenance entity modelling and visit-time display refresh remain separate work. The scope has not expanded to all objects or ten million downloads.

## Import safeguards and verification

The new offline batch is SHA-256 pinned and uses the existing exact-identity indexes for source scheme/object ID, canonical/source URL and institution/accession. Names or titles alone never merge records. All 41 painter authorities must already exist; no painters are created. The import runs as a local-only serializable transaction with an advisory lock and statement timeout. Default mode executes the same transaction and rolls it back. New receipt files are exclusive-create.

Missing material, dimensions, aliases, accession and explicitly sourced creation-place text are filled only in draft/review records. Existing nonempty values, titles and dates are not overwritten. Replaying the same batch does not repopulate an editor-cleared field or re-add a removed highlight. Owner selections and public visibility remain untouched. Rights decisions stay metadata/manual-review; the importer does not fetch assets or activate connectors.

All Go packages pass database-backed tests, and go vet passes. The two added deep-batch tests cover the exact snapshot, every date and source identity, rollback, cross-version accession matching, enrichment preservation, cleared fields, removed highlights, archived-record conflicts and museum API counts. Existing European tests remain passing. An isolated fixture with 100,000 unrelated artworks, identifiers and citations uses the exact identity indexes with no sequential scan. This is not a ten-million-row load benchmark or production throughput guarantee.

The final local dry-run left 17 checked data/audit counts unchanged. Apply created 155 works and 19 institutions, enriched two existing works, added 17 museum highlights and 338 citations, and recorded 185 accepted ingestion rows in a needs_review job. Acceptance is not publication. Applied replays of both the new 157-record and old 59-record snapshots left the same checked counts unchanged. API checks confirm the Louvre's 13 total Artline works/10 new highlights, Ireland's seven works/seven highlights, the Goldfinch undated group, and unchanged public/preview authorization boundaries. A private pre-import PostgreSQL backup was created and its archive listing verified; full restore rehearsal was not performed.

The companion machine-readable inventory contains every source URL and the complete selected evidence fields. The applied receipt supplies exact local artwork IDs. Historical research artifacts and the original 59-record snapshot are preserved, not rewritten to suggest they were generated after this expansion.
