# Review of all historical presets — 22 September 2026

Implemented a subject/region review of every All starting point. Before this review, only French and Russian Revolution had explicit focus; the other 28 were date windows. All 30 now declare their scope and explain it in About this period.

## Rules applied

- 22 presets use recorded regional associations; eight intentionally retain worldwide artworks/books: Empire, Modernism, First World War, Interwar, Second World War, Decolonization, Cold War and Digital. Global events are not forced into a European country list.
- Eleven thematic presets use explicit event sequences. This prevents unrelated same-country events from dominating Space Age, Civil Rights and similar views.
- Core books/events explicitly linked to a preset are local highlights. Their global `top100` flag is unchanged. Context entries keep the ordinary Highlights filter. Up to three contextual events are labelled; a date window may exclude some of them.
- Geography supplements explicit subject links. Modern place names are search associations, not a claim about ancient national identity. Same years do not establish influence, and a regional setting is not a subject/style attribution to every artwork.
- Byzantine tradition values are exact, reviewed object metadata. Anonymous/unlinked creators remain eligible; current museum country does not supply origin.
- All existing publication/review rules, dates, source-backed art eligibility, the 1970 artwork cutoff and mandatory renderable pictures remain enforced. User geography/search and native filters intersect the preset. Highlights is on by default and switchable.
- Every link was checked against an existing record. No catalogue inserts, date changes, publication, image downloads, commits or deployment were performed in this review.

## Findings and remaining catalogue gaps

- Sahel currently returns the Mali Empire event and no highlighted illustrated art/books. The historical Ghana Empire is not mapped to modern Ghana. Ghana/Songhai coverage needs sourced records, not unrelated European fillers.
- Writing, Classical, Buddhism and Islamic Learning have no illustrated artworks satisfying default highlight/eligibility rules in these snapshots. Mughal and Edo have no highlighted books in their context windows. Turning Highlights off still works; these are catalogue coverage gaps, not permission to relax dates, evidence or picture requirements.
- The First Buddhist Council record has no date. It remains unplaced. No complete Song dynasty, Ashoka or Silk Road event was fabricated to fill coverage.
- Renaissance and Romanticism event records, the Secret History of the Mongols, Galileo and Internet milestones now survive preset Highlights even where global flags or country metadata were missing.
- Byzantine results include recorded anonymous mosaics and icons held abroad, including Martyrdom of St. Lawrence and the Hagia Sophia Gabriel mosaic. Their recorded creator labels and provenance are preserved.
- Space Age uses Sputnik and flown Apollo missions, including the flown Apollo 13 mission. Cancelled Apollo 18–20 are excluded. [NASA’s Apollo programme](https://www.nasa.gov/the-apollo-program/) supplies the mission reference; listing a flight does not imply a Moon landing.
- Digital has existing Internet (1969) and Internet Protocol (1981) events. A World Wide Web milestone is still absent; [CERN’s history](https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web/) was reviewed, but no placeholder or date was invented. A main-period window beginning in 1970 correctly excludes the point-dated 1969 event.
- Source links now point to subject-specific museum/library material instead of generic collection landing pages. Some Library of Congress pages were available through indexed text but blocked direct retrieval (403); no full-page inspection is claimed for those links.
- Existing record dates remain authoritative for rendering. Further bibliographic dating work, including the Principia record’s current 1680 value, remains separate from preset selection.
