# Jean-Honoré Fragonard — illustrated catalogue pass, 11 October 2026

The request was “find pictures for Jean-Honoré Fragonard — we can cover all pictures!”
This pass delivered **46 verified primary images** to production: **35 existing
image gaps filled and 11 new illustrated review artworks**. The artist now has
**323 active catalogue records, 236 illustrated and 87 with explicit image gaps**,
up from 312 records and 190 images. This is catalogue coverage, not a claim to
have illustrated Fragonard's entire oeuvre.

[Public artist catalogue](https://artlines.org/artists/jean-honore-fragonard-q127171)
· Artist ID `18f1c6ba-4184-4546-b5b8-dddcec11ded2`

The real local catalogue was never connected to or modified. Production image
attachments and selected new review works were authorized by the request and
standing illustrated-expansion instructions. No deployment or commit was made.

All 46 delivered reproductions were individually viewed and technically checked.
Their full source frames were proportionally resized and JPEG-compressed to at
most 100,000 bytes. Create-only cloud uploads were verified against their public
byte hashes. Existing images were retained. The original 190 image records were
checked for changes, and local image hashes supported duplicate screening;
this does not claim every original image was visually inspected.

The Rijksmuseum selection uses exact native object identifiers, inventory
numbers, titles and explicitly uncropped downloadable image identities, with
native Public Domain Mark evidence. It includes two separately catalogued verso
drawings: these fill existing image gaps and are not counted as new objects.

Paris Musées delivery uses the exact first-canvas CC0 photographs for PDUT1194
(Jérôme de Lalande), J 29 (Perrette) and P2100 (L'Amour triomphant). Other canvases'
licences were not generalized to the selected photographs or vice versa. The
Met photograph uses archived exact object API evidence for accession 2006.467,
object 372973, retaining its credit and qualified after-Preti attribution. No
request was made to the held Met object/API surfaces to obtain this image.

## WikiArt reconciliation

The fresh English text index and French source API yielded **70 distinct current
artwork URLs**, all captured and reconciled. A cached search result advertised
74; that discrepancy is retained rather than converted into four assumed works.

| Final outcome among the 70 source entries | Count |
| --- | ---: |
| Already illustrated catalogue work; original retained | 41 |
| Image added to an existing catalogue work | 4 |
| New illustrated review work | 11 |
| Unresolved; no production addition | 14 |

The four existing matches are The Seesaw (Thyssen), The Reader / L'Étude
(Louvre MI 1059), The Cradle (Amiens MPLav1894-143) and The Birth of Venus
(Grobet-Labadié GL578). Existing dates and attribution history were preserved,
including the unknown catalogue date for The Seesaw and the earlier Taraval
attribution evidence for The Birth of Venus.

The eleven new paintings are Young Woman Playing with a Dog; Portrait of
François-Henri d'Harcourt; The Watering Place; The Happy Lovers; Aurore; The
Little Sultan; The Laundresses; Self-Portrait (1760–1770 source range); The Small
Park; A Young Scholar; and The Beginnings of Model / Les débuts du modèle.
They retain source titles and documented date precision. All remain in review.
No source location was automatically accepted as a holding or current-display
record. Personal survey selections are recorded separately from museum
masterpiece designations.

Notable identity and date decisions:

- Harcourt differs from the existing Louvre fantasy portrait; the suspect
  WikiArt Alfort museum label remains source evidence, without a new holding.
- The Watering Place is not silently merged with the Lyon versions, and The
  Laundresses (Saint Louis) differs from the Rouen composition.
- The Small Park painting differs from existing prints and a Morgan watercolor.
- The new Self-Portrait differs from the Lowendhal portrait despite a close
  perceptual-hash result. Flagged pairs were reviewed visually.
- The Happy Lovers retains WikiArt's 1760–1765 range with the Norton Simon
  circa 1751–1755 discrepancy documented. A Young Scholar likewise retains
  the WikiArt 1775–1778 date and the conflicting Wallace circa 1765–1775 evidence.
- WikiArt leaves Les débuts du modèle undated. The exact
  [Musée Jacquemart-André page](https://www.musee-jacquemart-andre.com/fr/oeuvres/debuts-modele)
  supplies circa 1770, oil on canvas and 50 × 63 cm. Those museum fields are
  separately cited; the unknown WikiArt date remains in the source evidence.

The 14 unresolved source entries include unknown dates or physical versions,
suspected father/son creator conflations (Psyche and Her Two Sisters; François I
in the Studio of Rosso), and one poor reproduction with a marked magenta cast.
The Rest on the Flight into Egypt candidate differs from the Baltimore version
found during follow-up. The Useless Resistance candidate must be reconciled to
the rectangular FAMSF panel, not the Nationalmuseum oval canvas. At the Stove is
visually distinct from the existing Pushkin Preparation of Dinner. None of these
leads was turned into a duplicate or assigned an unsupported date.

The generic Joconde metadata licence was not treated as permission for every
museum photograph. Actual image credit and explicit permission requirements
remain recorded. WikiArt's user-approved source policy was applied separately;
missing independent rights clearance did not block selected WikiArt images.
Recorded access restrictions for the Met, Louvre and Wikimedia image host were
preserved. New denials from Norton Simon, Saint Louis and FAMSF were recorded
and not retried through alternate access routes.
