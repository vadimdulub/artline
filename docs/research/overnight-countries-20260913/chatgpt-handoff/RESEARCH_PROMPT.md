I am expanding Artline, a public, read-only research catalogue of painters and artworks. Records remain **In review** until editorial validation. Research additional real artworks and improve existing records using the attached CSV files. The completed baseline includes research in the Netherlands, Greece, Russia, France, Italy, Spain, Germany, Austria, Belgium and Finland, with a Portuguese research queue awaiting import. Start by resolving country gaps and Portuguese held identities, then run a fresh 20-round Dutch gap campaign and choose the next under-covered country using the inventory (for example Sweden, Denmark or the United Kingdom). Include museums and collections worldwide, including institutions not yet in the catalogue. Do not repeat completed scopes without a new research question.

The attached inventory is a duplicate-checking baseline, not a source of truth for missing or disputed facts. Country verification is the highest priority. An artist's documented national/cultural affiliation is separate from birthplace, citizenship at a particular date, and the country of the museum holding an artwork. Preserve multiple affiliations when supported, and explain historical context. Do not turn “Netherlandish” or “Flemish” into Dutch automatically. Do not turn “Russian Empire” or “Soviet” into Russian automatically. Ukrainian, Belarusian, Armenian, Georgian, Greek and other affiliations require their own evidence. Do not infer country from a name, language, place of birth, or museum address.

Perform at least 20 substantive research rounds per country, with distinct creator, institution or collection scopes. A round must investigate a concrete question and report sources, new findings, exclusions and unresolved issues. Twenty API pages or twenty batches of the same findings do not count as twenty research rounds. Prioritize gaps in the provided country/research files, women painters, less-covered creators, documented museum holdings, and Russian icons, Greek, Byzantine and post-Byzantine traditions. Museum holdings outside the artist's country are fully in scope.

Use museum object pages, official collection APIs and museum creator authorities as primary evidence. Wikipedia, Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons are valuable discovery and corroboration sources. Follow their references to museums and scholarship. Compare the latest museum attribution with Wikidata: a museum may reject an attribution that Wikidata still lists without qualification. Preserve labels such as attributed to, workshop of, circle of, follower of and formerly attributed to. Do not invent a named painter for an anonymous or workshop-attributed object. Conventional names such as Meister des, Meister von, Mestre dos or Master of can denote anonymous masters; distinguish these from named painters such as Maestro Bartolomé. Priority Russian icons, Greek, Byzantine and post-Byzantine works can remain linked to a documented anonymous-master or workshop identity.

Artworks must have been created in or before 1970 and be supported by a documented museum connection or a clearly sourced masterpiece/highlight designation. The cutoff is the artwork's creation date, not the artist's life dates. Keep museum-designated highlights separate from personal selections. A holding institution does not prove that a work is currently on view. Only report on-view status when a fresh, dated display record explicitly supports it.

An unknown creation date may be retained as a metadata research candidate with cutoff_eligibility=needs_review; it must not be promoted to eligible just because its artist died before 1970. A broad source range mirroring the artist lifespan needs independent date evidence.

Do not confuse a sitter, collector, donor or owner with the creator. “Unknown” in an artwork title often describes the sitter; do not exclude a work with a named creator on that basis. Consult country_source_evidence.csv by artist_slug when a profile's summarized evidence URL list is incomplete.

Missing details are acceptable: retain a source-backed artwork with a reliable creator link even when the title, precise date, type, biography or image is not available. Never invent a year, title, museum object number, country, biography or current display claim. A century is not an exact year. A range crossing 1970 is unresolved for eligibility. Preserve uncertainty, circa qualifiers, alternative dates and the original source wording. Use an explicit title statement where a Wikidata label is merely “-” or an identifier; do not mistake a placeholder for a title.

Read README.md and manifest.json first. The CSV is a dated local snapshot; some later local changes are pending production authentication. Use stable slugs, not UUIDs, to match across environments. Blank production IDs mean unverified, not absent. Follow canonical_redirects.json and never propose an archived duplicate as a new object. artworks_added_this_session includes gross inserted rows, including replacements of older placeholders and later archived duplicates; it is not a count of newly discovered physical objects. Country files overlap when an artist has several documented affiliations. image_delivery_state identifies local-only images; a blank public image URL does not mean that no local image exists.

Before proposing a new artwork, compare its source object IDs, Wikidata ID, accession number **within the correct institution**, source URLs, creators, dates and images against the baseline. Same-title paintings, studies, copies, versions, companion panels and cropped details are not automatically duplicates. A loan can produce records at two institutions for one physical object. Two Wikidata IDs can also describe the same physical object. Mark such cases for review with evidence; do not silently merge them. Also search creator-plus-inventory and exact source object URLs when an existing record has no institution link. A missing institution is not proof of a new object. Finnish National Gallery Wikidata P9834 is a numeric artwork ID; P4177 is a legacy artist identifier and must not be equated to current numeric FNG person IDs. A shared acquisition-register number is not necessarily a unique physical inventory. Recto and verso can be distinct catalogue works on the same support. A triptych and its separate panels remain distinguishable. For painters, check aliases, transliterations, exact authority IDs and compatible life dates before creating a new identity. A matching name alone is insufficient. A work linked only through formerly_attributed_to must not inherit that painter's country. Two Walraven-linked works in this baseline intentionally have blank artwork country because the museum rejected the attribution; preserve the historical link and investigate the current maker separately.

For images, provide authentic reproductions of the specific artwork. Prefer museum public-domain/CC0 images or Commons files with clear per-file public-domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA evidence. A museum API's metadata licence does not automatically license its images. Supply the image source page, direct image URL, exact licence name and URL, credit, attribution requirements and rights evidence. Flag crops, details, photographs of frames, watermarks, blurred images and disputed artwork identities. Do not generate substitute artwork images or download whole catalogues. An image without verified rights must remain a metadata-only lead.

Deliver UTF-8 CSV files with one row per proposed physical artwork, plus separate painter-country and duplicate-review CSVs. Keep cells as literal data, preserve Unicode, and use blank cells for unknown values. Separate source facts from your inferences. Keep all proposed records in review.
