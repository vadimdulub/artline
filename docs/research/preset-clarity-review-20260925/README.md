# All preset review — 25–26 September 2026

All **30 starting categories now include real artworks with images** under their default filters. This pass adds 26 selected artwork records/images: 12 from the Arab region, 12 for four empty historical categories, and two documented visual roots of computer art. All 26 remain local review records; no existing record was deleted or published.

## Selection changes

- **Silk Roads:** focuses on overland routes through Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, China, Mongolia, Afghanistan, Pakistan, India, Iran, Iraq, Syria and Türkiye. Italy/Greece are removed from this starting focus. This describes the selected overland view, not the full historic reach of trade. Geography follows [UNESCO's Silk Roads cities](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/silk-road-themes/cities-silk-roads), [Zarafshan–Karakum corridor](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1675/) and [Chang'an–Tianshan corridor](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1442/). Six selected travel/knowledge books replace 57 geographic matches. The final artwork count is 68 after adding three Buddhist route objects.
- **Islamic worlds:** 44 illustrated museum objects, including the 12 additional Egyptian, Syrian and Iraqi objects. Arabic calligraphy and regional craft traditions remain distinct from ethnicity and religious assumptions about their makers.
- **Byzantium:** exact recorded Byzantine traditions and a smaller core geography replace a broad Italy/Egypt/Syria catch-all. Anonymous and workshop objects remain supported. Its 93 artworks are not restricted by a hard modern-country default that would discard objects of unknown origin or works held abroad.
- **Reformation, Romanticism, Modernism, Interwar:** defaults use reviewed lists of 6, 10, 15 and 14 artists respectively, with selected book authors included so the creator filter preserves literature. Highlights are off. Period-level selections describe artistic context rather than asserting every artwork depicts an event. Sources include the Met on [Reformation](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-reformation), [Romanticism](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/romanticism), [Cubism](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/cubism), and [MoMA on Surrealism](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/surrealism).
- **French Revolution:** keeps France and the reviewed creator list. **US civil rights:** keeps the United States, 16 selected Black artists and five selected writers, Highlights off; unrelated Thomas Weeks Barrett remains excluded. **Renaissance:** keeps a regional Highlights view as requested.
- **Mughal:** removes the unrelated First Opium War from context. Hamzanama is excluded from this preset pending reconciliation of the sixteenth-century Akbar project with the catalogue's nineteenth-century Ghalib Lakhnavi creator link. The original record remains untouched in review. Evidence: [V&A Mughal arts](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-arts-of-the-mughal-empire), [Columbia Hamzanama](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/hamzanama/).
- **Writing, classical antiquity, Buddhism, Sahel:** three source-verified objects each fill the previous empty artwork layers. Writing and classical use exact artwork IDs without modern-country defaults that would exclude unknown ancient origins. The Sahel currently has no selected book; that gap remains honest.
- **Digital:** the user approved documented visual roots. Only the two paintings with direct evidence of influence on Nake/Noll enter the artwork layer. Dates and medium remain unchanged; the wider context begins in 1916 while the main period remains 1970–2000.
- Every preset now selects its **books and events explicitly**, preventing unrelated works from entering solely through a country match. Existing carefully curated modern-event creator lists remain. Historical labels remain in source data but are removed from modern-country defaults. Türkiye defaults now match the actual country name.

The starting cards show main period dates and concise geographic/topic scope. The expanded details separately explain the wider context; repeated long country lists are removed. Artwork cards remain compact: one title line with full-title hover and one creator line.

## Verification and limits

- Database-enforced read-only audit of all 30 categories passed: no empty artwork category, every returned artwork has an image and ends by 1970, selected books/events stay in their reviewed lists, country defaults preserve their context, and pages remain bounded to 150 items. No fixtures were inserted and no test database was created.
- Atlas and HTTP API unit tests passed. The configuration test now accepts exact artwork selections as a legitimate scope, as used for the digital roots.
- All 30 starting buttons, country/creator defaults, Highlights, the visible focus and the first rendered image passed the browser test. Desktop/mobile starting list, keyboard/accessibility checks, switching presets, reset persistence and civil-rights filters passed. The first all-category attempt timed out while the first view was loading; an isolated rerun passed all 30 in 30.7 seconds with unchanged production code.
- The new historical/digital and expanded Islamic galleries are checked at 1440px and 390px for loaded images, full-title hover, no page overflow, and correctly loaded date/licence/source drawers. Proof images remain in `/tmp/artline-category-*`, `/tmp/artline-arab-capital-*` and `/tmp/artline-every-category-*`.
- TypeScript, targeted ESLint and the five starting-point frontend unit tests passed during this review. `git diff --check` and Python syntax validation are also checked at completion.
- Preserved `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` plans cover Civil Rights, French Revolution, Science, Renaissance, Silk Roads, Islamic worlds and Byzantium. Maximum measured statement execution was 335.372 ms on this real local catalogue. This is **not** proof of 10-million-row performance; representative large-scale load testing remains required before claiming that capacity.

`presets-before.json` and `*-before.json` preserve the baseline; `*-after.json` is the intermediate geographic/book review; `presets-final.json`, `*-final.json` and `final-plans/` preserve the completed state. Import evidence is in the sibling [Arab-region](../arab-world-images-20260925/README.md), [historical gaps](../preset-artwork-gaps-20260925/README.md) and [digital roots](../digital-art-roots-20260925/README.md) directories. New records are in review and therefore available through the local research preview, not newly published to the public catalogue.
